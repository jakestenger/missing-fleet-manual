## The finding this sweep existed for

### A 4.90.0 reconciler bug that silently skipped a fixed set of hosts

**Stated**, from the diff of `server/datastore/mysql/apple_mdm_batched.go` between the two tags, the fix's own source comment, and the 4.90.1 changelog.

The Apple profile and declaration reconcilers walk MDM hosts in pages of 5,000 (`reconcileAppleProfilesBatchSize = 5000`), ordered by UUID, carrying a cursor between pages. At 4.90.0 the page-exhausted test counted rows **after** collapsing duplicate UUIDs, and that collapse happens after the SQL `LIMIT`. The fix's comment states the consequence directly: "a full page can come back shorter than batchSize, and treating that as the end of the host universe would wrap the cursor early and starve every host later in the UUID ordering."

**Derived**, and this is why it belongs in a troubleshooting chapter: the failure is silent and stable. Affected hosts are never considered, so no command is enqueued, no `host_mdm_apple_profiles` row leaves NULL, nothing is marked `failed`, and nothing is logged. UUID ordering does not change between runs, so the same hosts are skipped every time. It presents as a group of Macs that simply never get profiles while the rest of the estate is healthy, which is exactly the shape an administrator will attribute to scoping or to the devices.

Both conditions are required: more than 5,000 Apple MDM hosts, and at least one duplicate host UUID. Declarations paginate through the same helper and were affected identically.

Added as a callout in §8.8.7 with the version check placed before anything else in the section, because every other procedure in that section would come back clean.

The same 4.90.1 diff also adds an `EXISTS` guard on `host_mdm.enrolled`, which is the changelog's separate fix for the reconciler queueing profiles for deleted hosts. Not written into the chapter; recorded here.

## Third confirming review, 2026-08-27: NOT READY, applied

No new defect from the previous corrections, and the ADE push-time and manual-enrollment additions were confirmed against the tag. What remained was **a remnant of the original queue finding that three rounds had not caught**, and it is worth recording because the correction itself was the thing that hid it.

**`NotNow` is not terminal, and the corrected queue rule said it was.** The chapter had been fixed to say Fleet selects rows that are `active = 1` **and have no result yet**, which is right for `Acknowledged` and `Error` and wrong for `NotNow`. The selection query joins results on `r.status != 'NotNow'`, so a row whose only result is a deferral still matches `r.status IS NULL` and **is offered again**. That is the entire purpose of `NotNow`: the device is locked or at the login window and is saying ask me later.

So the rule is `active = 1` and **no terminal result**, and the difference matters at the moment a reader is looking at a stuck command. Under the old wording, a `NotNow` row looked like an answered command that would never be selected again, which is the opposite of what is happening.

Applied in four places: hop 7 now distinguishes terminal from deferred, the queue rule says "no terminal result", the state table marks `Acknowledged` and `Error` as terminal and adds the `NotNow` with `active = 0` row that should not occur, and §8.8.13's "with no result" branch is split three ways.

Also added, because it is what a reader should watch: Fleet records the first deferral in `not_now_at` and counts them in `not_now_tally`, so a climbing tally is a command being delivered and refused rather than one that is stuck. And there is a mode in which Fleet does skip `NotNow`-answered commands, which is why a deferred row that has stopped being offered is a finding rather than the ordinary case.

**The two voice items are done.** "Every Apple MDM symptom is which hop stopped" was broader than the command-delivery model that follows it, and the chapter has four sections that are not command delivery; it now says most rather than every and names them. The no-payload explanation appeared at both 8.8.0 and hop 3; hop 3 now points back rather than restating.

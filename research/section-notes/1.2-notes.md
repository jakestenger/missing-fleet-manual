## 2026-08-25 review pass

Independent review on `gpt-5.6-sol`.

### Corrected: the agent options example was missing a required level

As published:

```yaml
agent_options:
  options:
    config_refresh: 60
    distributed_interval: 30
```

`docs/Configuration/yaml-files.md:296-305` shows `options` nested under `config`. Copied as
written, the block would not apply.

Checked whether the mistake propagated. One other `agent_options` block in the manual has a
direct child that is not `config`, at `8.2-log-surfaces.md:393`, and that one is correct:
`docs/Configuration/agent-configuration.md:314-332` documents `agent_options.orbit` exactly that
way and explains that it is deliberately kept outside the osquery flag schema.

**Which is the useful finding.** The nesting is not uniform: what osquery consumes goes under
`agent_options.config`, and what Fleet itself acts on, `update_channels` and `orbit`, sits
directly under `agent_options`. The chapter now says so, because a value written at the wrong
level is silently ignored rather than rejected.

| Placement | Examples | Consumed by |
|---|---|---|
| `agent_options.config.options` | `config_refresh`, `distributed_interval` | osquery |
| `agent_options.update_channels` | `orbit`, `osqueryd`, `desktop` | Fleet |
| `agent_options.orbit` | `debug_logging` | Orbit |

### Corrected: the rollout table denied inventory to all MDM-only hosts

As published, "MDM, no fleetd" listed "Reports, vitals, inventory, scripts, Orbit installs" as
unavailable, and the prose said such a host "is invisible everywhere else".

That is true where fleetd is the source of inventory and false where MDM is.
`server/datastore/mysql/apple_mdm.go:1427-1482` creates and updates Apple host records through
the MDM path, with iOS and iPadOS inventory arriving by MDM refetch command;
`server/datastore/mysql/android.go:38-145` creates Android host records populated from the
Android path. Those platforms have no agent to install, so an MDM-only host there is fully
visible.

The chapter also established this itself, in its own account of iOS, iPadOS and Android, so the
table contradicted the prose around it. Now split by platform: MDM-only is thin on macOS and
Windows, and normal on the platforms where MDM is the inventory source.

### Recorded, not fixed

- The five-channel model is presented as universal, but Linux has no MDM path and mobile and
  ChromeOS do not have the first four in this form. The review also notes that "genuinely
  independent" is contradicted a few lines later by the documented Windows case, where fleetd
  wakes an otherwise eight-hour OMA-DM poll
  (`docs/Contributing/architecture/mdm/windows-mdm-architecture.md:58-70`). Worth resolving when
  Part I is next revised; the model is load-bearing enough that changing it needs care.
- Host "online" status is computed from the shorter stored interval plus a sixty-second buffer
  (`server/fleet/hosts.go:1174-1192`) and is an osquery check-in signal. The chapter calls it a
  "reliable view of query and inventory availability", which claims more than the signal carries.
- The agent-update and hardware-mismatch material duplicates 3.7 and 8.4, and the
  hardware-migration path checked is macOS-only (`orbit/cmd/orbit/orbit.go:834-895`, guarded by
  `runtime.GOOS == "darwin"`) without the chapter saying so.

### A seventh correction, and one place where 1.2 was ahead of its neighbour

**Corrected:** the channel-change paragraph said hosts pick a change up "on their next thirty-second poll", which is the same unqualified cadence 3.7's review had just corrected. From fleetd 1.58.0 sustained server errors back the configuration poll off toward a five-minute ceiling, so thirty seconds is the healthy base rather than the interval.

**Worth recording because it runs the other way:** 1.2 already stated both `update_channels` transitions correctly, that removing the key reverts nothing and that a non-existent channel produces host-side errors with nothing server-side. **3.7, the owning reference chapter, was missing both**, and its first review flagged them as omissions. So the foundations chapter was ahead of the reference chapter on that mechanism, which is the opposite of the pattern the other six corrections show and a reason not to assume the direction of the error before checking.

### The correction I made here was itself wrong, and the review caught it in both places

Worth recording separately because it is the session's own pattern turned on its author. Applying 3.4's review, I corrected 1.2's capability table to say lock and wipe are MDM commands on macOS and Windows and scripts on Linux. **Windows lock is a script**, gated on scripts being enabled, and only Windows wipe is an MDM command. I generalised from the Linux finding to a two-way split that does not exist, and wrote the wrong version into the chapter and into this ledger's correction table.

Part I's review flagged both, naming the ledger row explicitly. That is the argument for the rule added to CONTRIBUTING today, that a ledger row is corrected in place rather than appended to: an appended entry that is wrong is as durable as the original claim, and this one would have outlived the chapter text it justified.

## Part I re-review, 2026-08-27

The chapter was reviewed on its own for the first time since the Part III work started. It returned NOT READY with ten factual defects, and the shape of them is worth reading before the list: **almost every one is a true statement about a fleetd Mac promoted to a universal.** The chapter had drifted into describing Fleet rather than describing the platform it was actually looking at.

### Corrections applied

| Claim as written | Why it was wrong | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| "Query and inventory work belongs to osquery" | True on fleetd only. The Chrome extension answers Fleet's distributed endpoints from its own virtual tables with no `osqueryd` present, and iOS, iPadOS and Android inventory arrives over the management channel. Now scoped, with the redirect spelled out | `server/service/osquery.go` endpoints shared by the extension; Android and Apple host creation paths |
| "Five genuinely independent channels" | Neither universal nor exhaustive nor fully independent. Linux has four, mobile has one, ChromeOS serves the osquery-shaped endpoints from one extension, and carving adds a sixth endpoint on hosts that use it. Rescoped to "a fully enrolled Mac or Windows computer", with an applicability matrix by platform and a definition of *independent* as separately diagnosable | Per-platform enrollment paths; `orbit/pkg/osquery/flags.go` carver endpoints |
| "One channel can affect another, in exactly one direction" | Two dependencies, not one, and the count was never established. The Windows MDM wake is one; a synchronous script run is refused for a host Fleet considers offline, which is an osquery judgement, is the other | `server/service/scripts.go` online check before synchronous runs |
| "Online gives a reliable view of query and inventory availability" | It does not. Any authenticated osquery request stamps last seen, config and logger included, so a host with a broken distributed channel stays online while every live report against it fails. This is the single most expensive defect in the chapter | `LEAST(distributed_interval, config_tls_refresh) + OnlineIntervalBuffer`, `server/datastore/mysql/hosts.go` |
| `distributed_interval` "commonly 10 to 30 seconds" | A claim about deployments, not about Fleet. Fleet's own default agent options set it to 10, and fleetd does not pass the flag at all | `server/datastore/mysql/schema.sql` default `app_config_json`; `orbit/pkg/osquery/flags.go` |
| "If Orbit is not running, neither osquery nor Fleet Desktop are running" | Supervision model, not invariant. Orbit kills pre-existing `osqueryd` processes at startup precisely because killing Orbit on Windows orphans them | `orbit/cmd/orbit/orbit.go`, the `KillAllProcessByName("osqueryd")` call and its comment |
| Cooldown is per artifact "so everything else carries on" | The cooldown is per target, but the *pass* is not. The runner returns on the first lookup or update error, so a stuck Orbit target stops osquery and Desktop from being checked at all. Corrected here and in 3.7, which had inherited it | `orbit/pkg/update/runner.go` returns inside the target loop; target order in `orbit/cmd/orbit/orbit.go` |
| Hardware-migration self-recovery stated universally | The entire comparison is inside `runtime.GOOS == "darwin"`. Windows and Linux images carry the previous host's enrollment. Corrected here and in 8.4, which had inherited it | `orbit/cmd/orbit/orbit.go` |
| An option "silences enrollment error logging" | It suppresses one error-level call. Every failed attempt is still logged at info level, and the retry budget is six attempts at 10, 20, 40, 80, 160 seconds | `client/orbit_client.go` |
| Android server URL "cannot be changed afterwards" | Fleet accepts the change; only presence and well-formedness are validated, and this release's change handling resynchronizes Apple DEP profiles, not Google's push endpoint. The immutability is operational, and the failure mode is a quiet outage rather than a rejection | `server/service/appconfig.go`; `server/mdm/android/service/service.go` |
| "Anything involving the person belongs to Fleet Desktop" | Contradicted by the chapter's own later text. Orbit prompts for the LUKS passphrase itself through `zenity` or `kdialog` | `orbit/pkg/luks/luks_linux.go` |

### Unsourced claims removed or marked

"Most confusion, and most of the time spent in Part VIII" was a frequency claim about support cases that no checkout can establish; removed. "Nothing is dropped server-side" is now bounded to the agent's own queue with the wider guarantee marked unverified. "Nothing to see server-side" for a bad channel became "Fleet's console shows only the consequence". `edge` and `stable` are now described as repository conventions rather than as facts readable from the application source. "Both = everything in this manual" became the union of what the platform, enrollment type, configuration and edition allow.

### Licence gates the rollout table had suppressed

Every lock is Premium; Free's `LockHost` returns `ErrMissingLicense` unconditionally. Every wipe is Premium except an eligible company-owned Android device, which Free's `WipeHost` allows and rejects everything else for. Disk encryption, including the Linux LUKS escrow the table listed as a plain fleetd capability, is Premium. **This is the fourth chapter this session in which a licence claim was wrong**, and no checker catches them.

## Second round, 2026-08-27

NOT READY again. Nine of the ten original defects were confirmed resolved, and **the corrections produced seven new ones**, which is the clearest single demonstration of the pattern this project keeps hitting. The reviewer also retracted two of its own findings, which is worth recording because it cuts the other way.

### What the corrections introduced

| The new claim | Why it is wrong | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| "Anything osquery consumes sits under `agent_options.config`" | The opposite universal, written while fixing a different one. `command_line_flags` is osquery's startup configuration and is a **direct child** of `agent_options`, alongside `config`, `update_channels` and `extensions`. The real split is osquery's *runtime* configuration against its *startup* flags, not osquery settings against Fleet settings | `server/fleet/agent_options.go`: `Config` and `CommandLineStartUpFlags` are siblings |
| "Upgrade osquery without touching Orbit: the `osqueryd` key alone" | Operationally dangerous and it contradicted the row directly above it, which correctly said an omitted component means `stable`. Orbit normalises **every** omitted component to `stable`, so a block naming only `osqueryd` silently moves Orbit and Desktop to `stable` as well. A reader pinning osquery would have unpinned Orbit | `orbit/cmd/orbit/orbit.go`, `setStableAsDefault` |
| "Fleet does not control [MDM] timing. Apple and Microsoft do" | True of Apple, false of Windows. Fleet sets the Windows poll interval and can ask a capable fleetd host to open an OMA-DM session immediately when work is waiting, which is the same wake this chapter describes two paragraphs earlier | `docs/Contributing/architecture/mdm/windows-mdm-architecture.md` |
| Linux escrow covers "hosts that already had LUKS at install time" | Fleet checks the host's **current reported** encryption state, not its installation history, so a host encrypted after enrollment becomes eligible on its own. 3.4 already said this correctly | `server/service/orbit.go`: `RunDiskEncryptionEscrow` reads `host.DiskEncryptionEnabled` |
| "A Linux host without either dialog tool cannot [escrow], whatever else is installed" | **I knew this was wrong and wrote it anyway.** 3.4's own research established two escrow paths. snapd-managed TPM-backed FDE escrows the recovery key through the snapd socket **silently**, with the dialog used only best-effort to warn on failure; the dialog is fatal only on the passphrase path. Both need `cryptsetup`. Now a two-row table | `orbit/pkg/luks/luks_linux.go`: `Detect` then `runRecoveryKeyEscrow`, before the `notifier == nil` check |
| "Nothing on the host will notice" a hardware migration off macOS | The opposite universal again. The source establishes that *this particular recovery check* is absent on Windows and Linux, not that nothing anywhere notices | `orbit/cmd/orbit/orbit.go`, the `darwin` guard |
| "It gives you certificates on Android" | **Eighth licence defect of the session.** Creating the certificate template the Android app delivers is refused on Free with `ErrMissingLicense` | `server/service/certificates.go` |

Also: the Fleet Desktop ownership rule was corrected in one place and survived in two others as "any dialog" and "the only end-user-facing surface"; 3.3 still said Windows has "the same two channels as every other agent platform" when Linux has none; and 3.4 called the osquery endpoints "separately authenticated" when all three use one node key.

### Two retractions by the reviewer

**"Online proves a recent authenticated request" was its own overstatement, and I had adopted it.** When no `host_seen_times` row exists Fleet substitutes `created_at`, so a newly created host reads as online for the grace period without having said anything. The opening now says so.

**"Leave both `IMAGE-OK` assets untouched" was withdrawn once the prose changed.** The five-channel diagram still renders "Five channels. They fail independently" and "every 10 to 30 seconds", both of which this chapter now retracts. The asset is marked `IMAGE-REGENERATE` with a corrected prompt, and a new checker, `build/check-image-regeneration.py`, makes that marker block a `verified` stamp. **An accepted image is accepted against the prose as it stood**, and no existing check noticed that a correction had invalidated one.

The planned Agent screenshot asked for update channels in a panel that shows component versions only; the brief now says so and says not to reinstate it.

## Third round, 2026-08-27

NOT READY, eight items. **The character of the findings changed, which is the useful result.** Rounds one and two found fabricated claims; round three found mostly *propagation* failures, corrections applied in one passage and not in the three others that carried the same claim. Only two findings were genuinely new discoveries.

### The two new ones

**There is a sixth traffic family and the chapter had never counted it.** Fleet Desktop talks to Fleet over its own `device/...` API, authenticated by a per-host token rather than a node key, which is what lets a person open My Device without a Fleet account. Fleet's own architecture drawing gives it an arrow alongside Orbit's and osquery's. It is now named and deliberately placed outside the five, since it is optional and carries the end user's session rather than management work, and 8.1 uses the same boundary. **Three rounds of arguing about whether the count was five did not surface that the list itself was incomplete.**

**Two more licence gates in the rollout table.** Uploaded software installation is Premium, and App Store or managed-app delivery is Premium. So a Free deployment gets the reporting half of that table and very little of the acting half. The chapter said "three licence gates" and named three; it is more than that, and the count has been removed rather than raised.

### The six propagation failures

| Correction | Where it had not reached |
|---|---|
| The `created_at` fallback, so a new host reads online without checking in | Fixed in the opening only; two later passages and 8.1 still said online speaks for osquery and nothing else |
| Fleet Desktop is optional | Fixed at the component list; the platform table still said "yes" for all three fleetd platforms |
| Windows lock is a script | Fixed in the ownership table; the rollout table still promised lock generically on an MDM-only host, where it cannot run |
| The Android URL is editable and breaks rather than being refused | Fixed in 1.2; **2.1, 2.6, 2.8 and 2.12 all still said it cannot be changed**, and 2.8's unrendered image prompt was going to bake the claim into a picture |

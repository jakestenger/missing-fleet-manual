### Corrected: the agent options example was missing a required level

As published:

```yaml
agent_options:
  options:
    config_refresh: 60
    distributed_interval: 30
```

`docs/Configuration/yaml-files.md:296-305` shows `options` nested under `config`. Copied as
written, the block would not apply.

Checked whether the mistake propagated. One other `agent_options` block in the manual has a
direct child that is not `config`, at `8.2-log-surfaces.md:393`, and that one is correct:
`docs/Configuration/agent-configuration.md:314-332` documents `agent_options.orbit` exactly that
way and explains that it is deliberately kept outside the osquery flag schema.

**Which is the useful finding.** The nesting is not uniform: what osquery consumes goes under
`agent_options.config`, and what Fleet itself acts on, `update_channels` and `orbit`, sits
directly under `agent_options`. The chapter now says so, because a value written at the wrong
level is silently ignored rather than rejected.

| Placement | Examples | Consumed by |
|---|---|---|
| `agent_options.config.options` | `config_refresh`, `distributed_interval` | osquery |
| `agent_options.update_channels` | `orbit`, `osqueryd`, `desktop` | Fleet |
| `agent_options.orbit` | `debug_logging` | Orbit |

### Corrected: the rollout table denied inventory to all MDM-only hosts

As published, "MDM, no fleetd" listed "Reports, vitals, inventory, scripts, Orbit installs" as
unavailable, and the prose said such a host "is invisible everywhere else".

That is true where fleetd is the source of inventory and false where MDM is.
`server/datastore/mysql/apple_mdm.go:1427-1482` creates and updates Apple host records through
the MDM path, with iOS and iPadOS inventory arriving by MDM refetch command;
`server/datastore/mysql/android.go:38-145` creates Android host records populated from the
Android path. Those platforms have no agent to install, so an MDM-only host there is fully
visible.

The chapter also established this itself, in its own account of iOS, iPadOS and Android, so the
table contradicted the prose around it. Now split by platform: MDM-only is thin on macOS and
Windows, and normal on the platforms where MDM is the inventory source.

### Recorded, not fixed

- The five-channel model is presented as universal, but Linux has no MDM path and mobile and
  ChromeOS do not have the first four in this form. The review also notes that "genuinely
  independent" is contradicted a few lines later by the documented Windows case, where fleetd
  wakes an otherwise eight-hour OMA-DM poll
  (`docs/Contributing/architecture/mdm/windows-mdm-architecture.md:58-70`). Worth resolving when
  Part I is next revised; the model is load-bearing enough that changing it needs care.
- Host "online" status is computed from the shorter stored interval plus a sixty-second buffer
  (`server/fleet/hosts.go:1174-1192`) and is an osquery check-in signal. The chapter calls it a
  "reliable view of query and inventory availability", which claims more than the signal carries.
- The agent-update and hardware-mismatch material duplicates 3.7 and 8.4, and the
  hardware-migration path checked is macOS-only (`orbit/cmd/orbit/orbit.go:834-895`, guarded by
  `runtime.GOOS == "darwin"`) without the chapter saying so.

### A seventh correction, and one place where 1.2 was ahead of its neighbour

**Corrected:** the channel-change paragraph said hosts pick a change up "on their next thirty-second poll", which is the same unqualified cadence 3.7's review had just corrected. From fleetd 1.58.0 sustained server errors back the configuration poll off toward a five-minute ceiling, so thirty seconds is the healthy base rather than the interval.

**Worth recording because it runs the other way:** 1.2 already stated both `update_channels` transitions correctly, that removing the key reverts nothing and that a non-existent channel produces host-side errors with nothing server-side. **3.7, the owning reference chapter, was missing both**, and its first review flagged them as omissions. So the foundations chapter was ahead of the reference chapter on that mechanism, which is the opposite of the pattern the other six corrections show and a reason not to assume the direction of the error before checking.

### The correction I made here was itself wrong, and the review caught it in both places

Worth recording separately because it is the session's own pattern turned on its author. Applying 3.4's review, I corrected 1.2's capability table to say lock and wipe are MDM commands on macOS and Windows and scripts on Linux. **Windows lock is a script**, gated on scripts being enabled, and only Windows wipe is an MDM command. I generalised from the Linux finding to a two-way split that does not exist, and wrote the wrong version into the chapter and into this ledger's correction table.

Part I's review flagged both, naming the ledger row explicitly. That is the argument for the rule added to CONTRIBUTING today, that a ledger row is corrected in place rather than appended to: an appended entry that is wrong is as durable as the original claim, and this one would have outlived the chapter text it justified.

## Part I re-review, 2026-08-27

The chapter was reviewed on its own for the first time since the Part III work started. It returned NOT READY with ten factual defects, and the shape of them is worth reading before the list: **almost every one is a true statement about a fleetd Mac promoted to a universal.** The chapter had drifted into describing Fleet rather than describing the platform it was actually looking at.

### Corrections applied

| "One channel can affect another, in exactly one direction" | Two dependencies, not one, and the count was never established. ### Licence gates the rollout table had suppressed

Every lock is Premium; Free's `LockHost` returns `ErrMissingLicense` unconditionally. Every wipe is Premium except an eligible company-owned Android device, which Free's `WipeHost` allows and rejects everything else for. Disk encryption, including the Linux LUKS escrow the table listed as a plain fleetd capability, is Premium. **This is the fourth chapter this session in which a licence claim was wrong**, and no checker catches them.

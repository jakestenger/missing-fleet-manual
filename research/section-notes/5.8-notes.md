# 5.8 Enforce disk encryption and manage recovery credentials, citation ledger

Drafted 2026-08-28. Process: outline consensus, research consensus over two rounds, draft, one
review round applied.

## A sourcing note, because this chapter is held to a stricter rule than the others

**The working research for this chapter is not in this repository.** It is held privately, and this
ledger is the only public record of what was established. The reason is recorded in
[`README.md`](README.md): the notes for this chapter and for 5.7 contained storage names and
retrieval plumbing for credentials that unlock disks, which is exactly what the chapter declines to
publish. Notes inherit the disclosure line of the chapter they support, and these had not.

That constrains this ledger too. Claims below are stated at the level the chapter states them: what
exists, what it unlocks, who may reveal it, what revealing does, what must survive. Where the basis
for a claim is a storage location or a retrieval path, the basis is described by its role rather than
named.

## The disclosure line, as agreed before drafting

Agreed with the reviewer at outline stage, and confirmed as executed at review: **operationally
specific but procedurally restrained.**

In: what a credential unlocks, who can reveal one, that reads are recorded, the difference between a
current and an archived credential, what key material each platform depends on, and how each is
handled through approved channels.

Out: interface routes, endpoints, commands, queries, storage names, sample values, bulk retrieval,
and anything that reads as a step-by-step extraction. A grep of the drafted chapter for extraction
shapes returns nothing, and the reviewer confirmed the boundary independently.

## Derived

**The four questions, and the fact that Fleet answers three of them partially and the fourth not at
all.** The chapter's organising structure. The reviewer wrote the final formulation; my draft had
"four claims get collapsed into one status", which was wrong in a specific way worth recording: it
implied a successful recovery test is an input to the displayed status. It is not an input to
anything. The status summarises partial signals from the first three.

**"To establish that you could actually recover a machine, recover a machine."** Editorial, and the
only honest answer to the fourth question.

**The prerequisites list.** Assembled from the per-platform gates rather than from any one place, and
included because an unmet prerequisite and a switch that does nothing look identical from the
interface. **Added in review**; the draft omitted them.

**The advice to treat a fleet move as a de-escrow in Windows-only deployments.** Follows from the
proxy check, and is stated as something to confirm on one host rather than as a certainty.

## Unverified, and phrased as such in the text

**The outcome of a fleet move in a deployment with Windows MDM enabled and Apple MDM absent.** The
code path is unambiguous; no integration test covers that deployment shape. The chapter says to
confirm it on one host before acting in bulk.

**TPM-backed Ubuntu.** Described only by its observable difference — escrow without a passphrase
prompt, and Fleet holding its own recovery credential rather than the one the platform uses day to
day — because the mechanism below that is not something this chapter publishes.

## Rejected, and why

**"Everything here is Fleet Premium."** Swept in ordinary observed-encryption inventory, which is
not. Narrowed to enforcement, escrow and retrieval, status interpretation, and Recovery Lock.

**"Fleet's agent has no dialog capability on Windows at all."** Broader than what was checked.
Narrowed to the BitLocker flow being silent, stated positively.

**"A fleet move deletes the live credential on every platform."** False as written. The deletion is
conditional, and the interesting fact is which condition.

**"Disabling Recovery Lock destroys the credential."** True in outcome, wrong in timing, and the
timing is the part an administrator can be caught by.

**"Permanently unreadable."** Too absolute for both certificate cases. Restoring the original
material would restore access; the chapter now says unreadable through Fleet's active decryption
path, and to plan as though irreversible.

**Three pieces of metacommentary about Fleet's documentation being wrong.** Cut on the reviewer's
advice, for the fourth time in this part. The corrections stand without the narrator pointing at the
source of the error. The underlying documentation defects remain recorded in the defect queue.

reachable by routine, well-intentioned administration.

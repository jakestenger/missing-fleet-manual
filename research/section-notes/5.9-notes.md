# 5.9 Automate responses to policy failures, citation ledger

Drafted 2026-08-28. Process: outline consensus in one round, research consensus over **two** rounds,
draft, one review round applied. Working research is held outside this repository in three files, see
[`README.md`](README.md).

## Why the title changed

The stub was "Automate remediation with policies". The reviewer's objection at outline stage was that
"remediation" names only the two responses that touch a host, and Fleet also offers webhooks,
tickets, calendar events and conditional access. The frontmatter title is now **"Automate responses
to policy failures"**. The filename is unchanged, deliberately, as in 5.7: renaming has broken links
in this project before.

## Why research took two rounds

The first round was reviewed against a research file that an agent was still writing. It grew from
about 990 lines to about 2,400 during the review, so round one covered roughly its first third and
the reviewer correctly reported host-side lifecycle behaviour as an unfilled placeholder. Round two
read the finished files.

**Twenty-eight corrections across the two rounds.** This ledger records the ones that changed what
the chapter says rather than how it says it.

A third research agent was killed mid-run and restarted. Its partial output was preserved rather than
overwritten, and the restarted agent appended its work as an explicitly independent second read. Two
of its findings changed the chapter after the review round, and both are recorded below.

## Derived

**The organising claim is the reviewer's**, proposed at outline stage: a policy automation is not a
general workflow engine, and is safe only when the response, the trigger mode and the policy's own
definition of success form a bounded loop.

**The three-part safety rule** in section three. My first version reduced it to writing the query
correctly, which the reviewer pointed out is only one of the three things the opening claims must
hold together. The final wording requires a repeat-safe response, a deliberate trigger choice, and an
observable definition of success, and says plainly that Fleet validates none of the relationships.

**"A script that ran successfully next to a policy that still says failing is the normal state of
affairs for the next hour."** Editorial, and the single most useful sentence in the chapter for
anyone operating this feature.

**The ranking in the escape-path section.** Assembled from mechanisms that are documented separately
and nowhere ranked.

## Unverified, and phrased as such

**The end-to-end consequence of cancelling an activated policy script.** The control flow is
unambiguous and the install path's contrasting guard makes it look like an oversight, but it was
reasoned from source rather than reproduced. The chapter states the behaviour; the defect entry says
it should be reproduced before filing.

## Rejected, and why

**"Scripts have no continuous cooldown"** as a defect. The reviewer showed the omission is
deliberate: integration tests require repeated runs, and the loop the cooldown exists to break cannot
form for scripts. It became a warning in the chapter instead. A later independent read then qualified
even that: the refetch flag is per host, so an install automation on the same host accelerates a
continuous script policy anyway. Recorded as C25 and left out of the chapter as too fine a point.

**"VPP installs are never retried."** Wrong, and both research agents made the same mistake by
looking for the wrong counter on the wrong table.

**"Fires once and then nothing happens again, forever."** Too absolute; several things re-arm it.

**"There is no pause."** Wrong. Turning scripts off globally is a genuine pause for script
automations, and the section that said otherwise was corrected after the review round rather than
during it.

**"Two of the seven responses fix the host."** An unstable count that disagreed with the chapter's own
table. Replaced with the category.

**"Installs a custom package you uploaded."** Omitted Fleet-maintained apps and contradicted 5.4.

**A false chronology.** I had written that a policy can install, ticket and block "in that order".
The real order is different and not one an administrator can rely on; the useful point was that the
responses do not coordinate.

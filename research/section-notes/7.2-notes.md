## Dependency and platform

MySQL and object storage backup mechanics are the vendor's. Fleet has no opinion and provides no
tooling, which is the honest hand-off rather than an omission.

## Operational practice

The recovery objective, the ordering of the two stores' captures, the Redis persistence decision,
**egress denial before starting a restored instance**, the verification sequence and the retention
coordination are all judgement.

**Egress denial is labelled as practice and given an actual allowlist** at review, rather than left as
an instruction to block things.

## Not established

Whether a restored instance's behaviour is identical across deployment shapes, since where installers
live and whether a replica exists are local choices. The chapter tells a reader to resolve the state
inventory against their own deployment rather than trusting a generic list.

## Rejected, and why

**A universal four-place inventory.** Replaced with a deployment-resolved one, because storage
placement is a deployment choice.

**"Starting empty loses only what is listed above."** It also loses transient coordination state,
including scheduler locks.

**"Retrieving one credential per platform proves recovery."** It proves the service can decrypt a
stored value on that chain. Not that the population is readable, and not that anything would unlock a
disk.

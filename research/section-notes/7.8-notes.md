# 7.8 Retire a Fleet deployment, citation ledger

Extracted 2026-08-30 from [`7.2-notes.md`](7.2-notes.md) (then numbered 7.3, "Back up, restore, and retire service state") when the retire section became its own chapter (P6 structural moves). The rows below moved with the section they support; drafting history is in the source ledger.

## Operational practice

The retirement order is judgement: the six steps, their sequence, and the warning that releasing devices and external assignments are the steps that hurt if skipped.

## Rejected, and why

**A missing retirement section.** Added: shutting Fleet down leaves devices enrolled, credentials
live and external assignments standing, and those are the steps that hurt if skipped.

## Per-platform release, added 2026-09-02 (queue R1-M14)

Step 2 ("release devices") had no platform mechanics and no verification criteria (morning-queue
finding). Added a "Release devices, per platform" section, sourced against fleet-v4.90.1:

- **Apple asymmetry**: `DeleteABMToken` (`server/service/apple_mdm.go`, free-tier stub returning
  `ErrMissingLicense`; real implementation `ee/server/service/mdm.go`) only deletes Fleet's local
  token record and flips the app-config flag when the count reaches zero — it never calls Apple.
  Confirmed no Apple API call anywhere in that function. This is why Apple needs two separate
  actions (unenroll/wipe the device, and remove the server assignment in Apple Business itself),
  unlike Android below.
- **Android symmetry**: `DeleteEnterprise` (`server/mdm/android/service/service.go`) calls
  `androidAPIClient.EnterpriseDelete` against Google's API *and* bulk-marks every Android host
  unenrolled in the same call — already documented in this book at 2.12 ("Turning Android MDM off
  deletes your enterprise"), cross-referenced rather than re-explained.
- **ChromeOS and Linux**: no new source research needed. ChromeOS has no MDM surface in Fleet at
  all (confirmed earlier for R1-M6: the extension only calls enroll/read/write, no config-fetch);
  release is entirely a Chrome-policy action in the Google Admin console, per 3.7. Linux has no
  MDM story either, so release is just fleetd/orbit removal (`it-and-security/lib/linux/scripts/uninstall-fleetd-linux.sh`
  in fleet-public: stop+disable the service, remove files, purge the package).
- **Windows and macOS**: cross-referenced this book's own existing content (2.11's "Turning
  Windows MDM off", already covers both halves in one script; 8.4.4's destructive `profiles
  remove` step) rather than duplicating it.

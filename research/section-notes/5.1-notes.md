## Derived

**The three-kind taxonomy** (desired state, discrete activity, device action) is the chapter's
organising model and is editorial. It was proposed by the reviewer during the structure
conversation and adopted because it predicts failure modes; Fleet does not present these as three
categories anywhere.

**"A change is not slow because Fleet is slow."** Editorial framing over the four channels'
verified behaviour.

**Rollout rings** are editorial method. Fleet has no ring feature; the chapter builds them from
labels.

**The repeatability argument** is editorial. The verified fact underneath is that the queue orders
work and does not deduplicate it.

**"Queued is not delivered."** Follows from activation handing work to a channel, but the framing
is mine.

## Corrected before drafting

The structure proposal I sent the reviewer said the queue had **four** activity types. It has five.
I had read the migration that created the table rather than the schema. `in_house_app_install` was
added by a later migration. This is now a standing rule in CONTRIBUTING.

The reviewer's own summary said Apple ADE re-enrollment "cancels all upcoming activity for the
host, regardless of whether past host activity is preserved". **Checked and narrowed:**
`ClearMDMUpcomingActivitiesDB` deletes only `vpp_app_install` and `in_house_app_install`. Scripts
and installer-based software survive. The chapter states the narrower, verified behaviour. A
reviewer's proposed wording is a claim, not a citation.

## Deliberately not stated

**No characteristic latency figures for the four channels.** The chapter says the waits differ in
length and does not put numbers on them, because the numbers are properties of Apple's push
service, a device's session schedule and Google's policy fetch rather than of Fleet.

**No claim about what happens to activated work on the device when a cancel is issued.** The
chapter says cancellation is a request rather than a recall, which is the honest statement of what
the source establishes. **Flag for a later round** if the channel-side behaviour turns out to be
determinable.

**No SQL and no table names in prose.** 8.6 owns the implementation-level account of this queue,
and STYLE forbids citing Fleet source files in the text. Part V explains consequences.

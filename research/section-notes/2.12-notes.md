# 2.12 Production readiness and handoff: citation ledger

Verified against `fleet-v4.90.1` (commit `dd0200f062`) in `~/Source/Fleet/fleet-public` on 2026-08-25.

This chapter is more synthesis than new product surface, since its job is to consolidate Part II. Every product claim is still verified below, and claims that are the book's judgement rather than Fleet's are marked as such.

## Backup and restore

**The central finding is an absence, so it was verified as an absence.** Searched `docs/Deploy/`, `docs/Configuration/` and `docs/Get started/` for backup and restore guidance. What exists:

| What Fleet says | Source |
|---|---|
| "It is always advised to back up the database before running migrations", linking to MySQL's own backup methods documentation | `docs/Deploy/Upgrading-Fleet.md:48` |
| Recommends RDS Aurora MySQL with AWS backups turned on | `docs/Deploy/Reference-Architectures.md:153` |
| `filesystem_max_backups` is unrelated: it is log file rotation for the filesystem logging plugin | `docs/Configuration/fleet-server-configuration.md:1653-1665` |

There is no backup procedure, no restore procedure, and no statement of what must be captured. The chapter says so plainly and describes it as a documentation gap rather than a product limitation.

The four complications the chapter raises are all synthesis over facts established elsewhere in the book, and are marked in the text as consequences rather than Fleet guidance:

- Only MySQL's loss is unrecoverable (1.6, verified there).
- The three stores cannot be captured at one instant (1.6).
- The server private key is outside the database and encrypts session tokens and escrowed LUKS data (2.11 and 2.9; `server/config/config.go:195-203`, `server/service/orbit.go:1506-1520`).
- An unrehearsed restore is a hypothesis. **Purely the book's position.** No Fleet source states it.

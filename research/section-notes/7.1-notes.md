# 7.1 Define the service operating model, citation ledger

Drafted 2026-08-28. Research took **two rounds**: the first came back NOT SOUND because the research
inventoried what Fleet exposes and never built the operating model the chapter is for. A second pass
built it and a second review cleared it with nine corrections. Draft, one review round applied.

Held to Part VII's four-class evidence rule ([`../part7-structure.md`](../part7-structure.md)).
**This chapter is the most judgement-heavy in the manual**, and the reviewer's standard for it was
that a judgement-heavy chapter earns its place by supplying decisions, tests and dependency
contracts, and becomes padding when it invents universal schedules or thresholds. The classes below
are marked accordingly.

## Operational practice

Nearly everything else, and deliberately: the five-outcome grouping, the objective record, the
ownership table, the operating calendar, the change gates and the runbook portfolio.

**The objective record gained three fields at review**: separate recovery-time and data-loss numbers
rather than one, how planned maintenance is treated, and the gap between the declared target and the
measured capability. That last field is the one the reviewer called the most important, because it is
the thing to either fix or stop claiming.

**The runbook portfolio was made conditional** at review. Several entries apply only if you have the
thing they are about, and **the Apple and Windows certificate renewals are separate runbooks rather
than one**, because 7.6 shows their consequences differ: one has a safe rollover path and the other
does not. An identity-provider outage and break-glass access were added.

## Not established

Fleet publishes no availability, recovery-time or data-loss target. The chapter says objectives are
yours and does not invent any.

## Rejected, and why

**"Seven indicators can carry an objective and eight cannot."** Too precise to be honest. Replaced
with a cost hierarchy, which is what actually decides whether an objective survives.

**"A server-version canary is architecturally impossible."** Overstated. There is no supported
schema-isolated one, which is a different and defensible claim.

**"Whoever owns the database owns Fleet's availability."** Rhetorically satisfying and wrong in
shape. Recast as shared accountability: the database team owns the component, you own the
consequence, and both need to know the consequence exists.

**"Ten deadlines Fleet stores without telling you."** A forced count. The chapter now scopes the few
surfaced dates and describes the silent classes without pretending to enumerate them.

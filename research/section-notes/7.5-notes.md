# 7.5 Maintain capacity and availability, citation ledger

Drafted 2026-08-28. Research consensus with corrections plus a gap round, draft, one review round
applied. Held to Part VII's four-class evidence rule
([`../part7-structure.md`](../part7-structure.md)). This chapter is where that rule does the most
work, because its most quotable numbers are the ones most easily mistaken for guarantees.

## Fleet contract, source checked at the tag

## Fleet reference deployment, tag checked

The reference architecture is an **example**. Its production Terraform has left the repository at this
tag, so its tuned values are **not auditable here**, and the chapter deliberately does not attribute
any to it.

## Operational practice, measured on Fleet's test deployment

Fleet's committed load-test artefacts record **a particular version, workload, duration and
infrastructure**. The reviewer was firm that they are a measurement of that deployment and never a
capacity guarantee, and the chapter cites them in those terms. A number without its four qualifiers
is how folklore starts.

## Dependency and platform

Redis eviction behaviour is **your Redis configuration's**, not Fleet's. **De-contracted at review.**
Database failover timing, object-store behaviour and load-balancer draining are likewise the
vendor's.

## Not established

Fleet publishes **no supported instance count, no recovery-time target and no sizing formula**. The
chapter opens by saying there is no universal capacity number rather than claiming to have none of
its own, which was the reviewer's wording.

## Rejected, and why

**"This chapter has no numbers to give you."** Slightly evasive. There is no *universal* number; the
constraints and defaults are real and are given.

**Summing the two connection pools into one per-instance figure**, and pointing that total at the
primary. They are separate ceilings against separate endpoints.

**"Nothing tells you" and "no timeout at any layer."** Both lost their Fleet-side scope.

**A draining instruction that leaned on a platform default.** Rewritten as Fleet's own behaviour plus
labelled practice, with the reader told to check what their platform actually does.

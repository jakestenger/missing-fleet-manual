## Derived

**"A finding is a conclusion from two inputs."** The chapter's organising claim. Each input is source-checked; framing the finding as their product, and observing that incompleteness in either shortens the list rather than erroring, is the manual's argument and the reason the chapter opens with the inventory switch rather than with vulnerabilities.

**The prioritisation order**, known-exploited then probability then reach then remediability, is editorial. The fields are source-checked; the ordering is judgement and is presented as such rather than as Fleet behaviour.

**"Version matching is the source of most false positives, and Linux distributions are where it bites."** The backporting problem is real and is why Fleet carries OVAL and goval data at all, but the word "most" is a frequency claim I have hedged into. **Flag for review**: either establish it or state the mechanism without the ranking.

## Deliberately not stated

**No claim about which feed covers which platform in detail.** The directory listing establishes that several sources exist; mapping each to its coverage needs a check per source that I have not done, and a wrong map here would be worse than the general statement that coverage differs.

**No claim about vulnerability history retention.** The agreed structure assigns exposure history to this chapter, and I have not verified the CVE dataset's retention or resolution. **Flag for review**: this is a named gap rather than an omission I am unaware of.

**No count of vulnerability sources.** The directory has entries that are not all feeds, so a count would be wrong in the way 4.1's detail-query count would have been.

## Boundaries held

Installing and updating software is Part V's, and the chapter hands over at the version rather than describing delivery. Patch policies are named and their trap repeated from 4.3, without re-explaining them. Single-host software absence goes to 8.4. Self-hosting concerns about feed reachability go to 2.9.

## Review round, 2026-08-27

NOT READY, fifteen required changes, all applied. Rewritten. Two findings corrected claims I had also written into other chapters.

### The thesis was one-directional and the failure is two-directional

I wrote that incomplete inputs make the conclusion "wrong in the direction that looks like good news". Stale inventory also **over-reports**: software uninstalled a week ago that Fleet still believes is present produces findings for risk that no longer exists. The chapter and the image brief now carry both directions.

### `software_updated_at` does not mean what I said, in two chapters

I described it as advancing when Fleet finishes reconciling a host's software. It advances when Fleet **changes** the stored software, inside the block that runs after an insert or delete. A host reporting the same software every hour leaves the field untouched, so three weeks old is consistent with perfect reporting and nothing installed. **Fleet exposes no host-level timestamp proving a successful unchanged refresh.** Corrected here and in 4.1's producer table.

### The rest

**Inventory disabled is not a silent clean page.** Fleet shows an explicit disabled state. And disabling collection is not deleting data: previously stored software stays.

**The setting is two-level.** Global, with a per-fleet features override, so "is inventory on" is a per-scope question. Added a procedure with that precedence and the dependency on the global vulnerability job.

**`disable_data_sync` is not "sync broken".** Five states look alike: downloading normally, externally provisioned deliberately, sync failing, local data failing to load, and matching failing. Only the first two are intended, and the second is indistinguishable from the third if the external process stops.

**Software and OS vulnerabilities are separate pipelines** with separate data and switches, including one that disables Windows OS vulnerability processing. They remediate differently, which is the reason the distinction belongs in a reading chapter.

**Mobile inventory was overgeneralised.** A personally owned iOS or iPadOS device reports only the apps Fleet manages, not its own; **iOS and iPadOS application vulnerabilities are not supported at this release**, so an empty mobile list is expected rather than clean.

**Six enriched fields, not three.** Publication date, description and resolved version join the three prioritisation signals. Only the three signals are Premium. **Free is not without ranking**: identifier, host count and detection date remain, which the draft told readers to ignore.

**A Premium null is not necessarily a sync fault**; it means no value is available, which may be an upstream gap. And **EPSS is a thirty-day prediction**, so a low score is not evidence that nobody is exploiting it, which is the reading my draft invited.

### Added on the completeness question

Vulnerability exposure history, which the agreed structure assigned here and my ledger flagged as a known gap: critical severity only, over a curated software set, about a month retained, seven days shown by default, and **the interface gates it on Premium** although the chart route itself has no separate licence check. A prioritisation and drill-down workflow ending at a version and a population. And the platform coverage map.

### Propagated

The patch-policy boundary went back into **4.3**: it is built from a Fleet-maintained app and is **Premium**, so it is not available for an arbitrary title you package yourself. My 4.3 draft said only that it must be fleet-scoped.

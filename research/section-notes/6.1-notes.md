# 6.1 Design safe automation and control change, citation ledger

Drafted 2026-08-28. Process: part-level structure agreed, research consensus over **two** rounds,
draft, one review round applied. Working research is held outside this repository, see
[`README.md`](README.md).

## Why research took two rounds

The first round returned SOUND WITH CORRECTIONS and, unusually, **not ready to draft**: three of the
six agreed sections were substantially unresearched. Path selection and ownership, the test ladder,
and recovery were all missing. A second agent researched exactly those, and corrected the reviewer
twice while doing it. Round two closed with "no further research round is necessary".

Twelve corrections came out of round one and twelve more out of round two. The review round then
found nine more. This ledger records the ones that changed what the chapter tells someone to do.

## Derived

**The three-problem separation** in the ownership section. My draft ran ownership, serialisation and
attribution together and concluded that one identity per writer solves them. The reviewer's objection
is exact: a separate identity solves only attribution. It neither prevents another credential writing
the field nor tells Fleet that one writer owns it. The chapter now names three problems and three
answers, and says plainly that the identity does none of the first two.

**One identity per automated *workflow*, not per tool.** Aligned with 2.6 at review. A single
workflow may reasonably use GitOps and direct calls together, and an identity per executable would
add noise without buying a boundary.

**The read-limited writer problem.** A GitOps identity narrow enough to be a safe writer may be
unable to read host details, activities or install results, which is the evidence its own change
worked. The chapter's answer is a second read-only identity rather than a wider writer. **Added at
review.**

**The pre-change contract.** Six things stated before the change: owner and identity, target and
intent, evidence, stop condition and decision-maker, correction, and correlation. **Added at review**,
because the chapter promised change control and was mostly supplying failure semantics. The last row
exists because Fleet records no run or request correlation marker of its own.

## Rejected, and why

**"Fleet has no idempotency mechanism."** Too absolute, and it contradicted the chapter's own later
and correct statement that retry safety is endpoint-specific.

**"Resource names within their fleet"** as a general business-key rule. Too broad to be safe.

**"Activities do not record reads."** False, and it contradicted 8.12 as corrected.

**"Four ways to change Fleet."** The opening said that and the table immediately said webhooks change
nothing. Now three inbound paths and one outbound.

**"An automation that nobody but its author can halt is a change-control problem wearing a technical
costume."** Clever, and it moved attention from the requirement to the author's verdict. Replaced
with the requirement.

**Five pieces of audience commentary**, on the reviewer's advice, of the "people assume otherwise"
and "the part people get wrong" variety. The same fault it flagged four times in 6.2.

## Held in the defect ledger rather than the chapter

The reviewer listed six mechanisms in round two that must not appear as actionable detail. Their
chapter-safe conclusions are the ones drafted: endpoint restrictions are supplementary; treat a
database backup as credential material; activities are a change log rather than an access log; never
emit secrets in host output; do not put receiver credentials in a webhook destination; and GitOps
mode and its exceptions are client-side governance rather than server authorization.

The evidence behind each is in `~/Vault/Claude Cowork/Work/General/Fleet bugs to file.md`, which is
outside this repository. S5 and S6 were recorded from this chapter's research.

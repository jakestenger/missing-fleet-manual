## Derived

**The five-step hierarchy** is the chapter's organising structure and is editorial. The ordering is
defensible from the mechanisms, but Fleet does not present these as a ranked list anywhere.

**"The mistake this chapter exists to prevent is reaching for the heaviest one first."** Editorial.

**"An override must return exactly the columns the original returned."** Follows necessarily from
the ingestion function being retained while the SQL is replaced. Stated as a rule because the
consequence of getting it wrong is silent.

**The uses and non-uses of `additional_queries`** are editorial. Fleet imposes no such distinction.

**The three questions to ask before writing an extension** are editorial.

## Deliberately not stated

**No extension-writing tutorial.** The structure conversation concluded that building osquery
plugins is too specialised to occupy a seventh of Fleet's reading half, and it is osquery's
subject rather than Fleet's. The chapter covers what Fleet does with an extension once it exists
and what you are signing up for, and sends the reader elsewhere for the code.

**No claim about where `additional` data appears in the interface.** The storage is established;
which surfaces render it was not checked. The screenshot brief asks for it, which will settle the
question when the screenshot is taken. **Flag for review.**

**No FIM or YARA configuration detail.** The keys are named as existing. Their semantics are
osquery's, and doing them properly is a chapter's worth of material that belongs with log
destinations rather than here.

**No TUF channel names.** Establishing which channels exist is a moving target and belongs with
agent updates in 3.7.

## Boundaries held

Query design and cost stay in 4.6 and are cited rather than repeated. Host record freshness stays
in 4.1. Log destinations go to 2.5. Managing any of this as code goes to 6.2. Agent updates and

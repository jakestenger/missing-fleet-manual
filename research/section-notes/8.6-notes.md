## Corrections made in this pass

### 1. "Thirty-seven schedules are registered", against a table of thirty-five

**Stated.** `server/fleet/cron_schedules.go` defines **34** `CronScheduleName` constants. `cmd/fleet/cron_registration.go` makes 36 register calls, but two of those are the mutually exclusive `vulnerabilities` paths (the real schedule and the remote-trigger proxy), and one registers a schedule whose name is a bare string literal, `mdm_service_discovery`, that never made it into the constant list.

Distinct registrable names: **35**. The chapter's own table has exactly 35 rows. The prose said 37, which is §26 twice over: a count the reader cannot reconcile with the list underneath it, and a count that is simply wrong.

Corrected, and the structure is now stated rather than just the number, since the structure is what a reader needs to reconcile the table against their own server: 34 in Fleet's list, plus one registered outside it.

Also added to the gating sentence: the two software migration schedules register only when a software installer store is configured. The chapter mentioned Premium and config gating and not this one.

### 2. Five schedules can be stamped `expired` while they are running

This is the material finding in this chapter, and it inverts the reading the chapter taught.

**Stated**, `server/datastore/mysql/cron_stats.go`. The reaper marks a `pending` or `queued` row `expired` when it is older than two hours **and** no unexpired row in `locks` has `l.name = cs.name`, or unconditionally at twelve hours. The source carries a `NOTE` saying what that means: schedules using `WithAltLockID` store their lock under a different name, "so the `NOT EXISTS` check won't find their lock and they fall back to the 2-hour timeout".

**Stated**, from grepping the option: five schedules do that. `cleanups_then_aggregation` locks as `leader`, `frequent_cleanups` as `leader_frequent_cleanups`, `integrations` as `worker`, `apple_mdm_worker` as `apple_mdm`, and `calendar` as `calendar`.

**Derived**, and the derivation is the point: for those five, a run still in progress at two hours is marked `expired` by the cleanup pass even though the owning instance is alive and working. The chapter previously said `expired` "means the opposite: nobody ever closed the row out, because the process that owned it went away without writing anything." That is one cause and it is now presented as the usual one rather than the only one.

It matters most where it is most misleading. `cleanups_then_aggregation` is the chapter's own "the big one", running roughly forty jobs on an hourly schedule, and it is exactly the schedule an operator inspects during a slow-Fleet incident. A pass exceeding two hours on a large estate is unremarkable, and its row will say `expired` regardless.

Added as a callout rather than a table footnote, because the wrong reading sends someone hunting a dead node.

### 3. A dynamic label's `platform` reaches far fewer hosts than its name implies

**Stated.** `server/fleet/labels.go` defines `ValidLabelPlatformVariants` as exactly five values: `""`, `darwin`, `windows`, `ubuntu`, `centos`. `server/datastore/mysql/labels.go` distributes a dynamic label's query with `WHERE (platform = ? OR platform = '')`, where the parameter is the platform string the host reported for itself.

**Stated.** `server/fleet/hosts.go` lists around twenty Linux platform strings a host can report, including `debian`, `rhel`, `sles`, `kali`, `gentoo`, `amzn`, `pop`, `arch`, `linuxmint`, `void`, `flatcar`, `coreos`, `zorin` and `cachyos`.

**Derived** from those two: a label with `platform: ubuntu` reaches only hosts reporting exactly `ubuntu`, and there is no value that means "Linux". The chapter had the correct five-value list and drew only the weaker conclusion, that `linux` matches nothing. The stronger and more damaging case is `ubuntu`, because it looks like it works and silently covers a fraction of the estate.

Three supporting facts added with it:

- **Stated.** A host reporting `rhel` is mapped to `centos` for label distribution when its OS version string contains "centos", so a `centos` label does reach those hosts. This is the one place the mapping is not identity, and it otherwise reads as an inconsistency.
- **A likely Fleet documentation error, and the eighth found by this project.** 4.90.0's release notes say Zorin hosts "can be targeted by label platform filters". `zorin` is not an accepted value for a label's `platform`, so that is not true of label platform filters. It is true of the **host list's** platform filter, which `server/datastore/mysql/hosts.go` does extend to `zorin`. Two unrelated things share the name "platform filter". Recorded rather than filed, per the standing instruction.
- **Stated.** A manual label rejects a `platform` value with a validation error rather than ignoring it.

## What this ledger does not certify

This is the part worth reading before trusting the chapter's reference tables.

**Column lists were spot-checked by hand, and then a checker was written for the checkable half.**

By hand, verified in full: `cron_stats`, `upcoming_activities`, `windows_mdm_responses`, `labels`, `host_mdm`. Verified in part: the `nano_*` set, the four per-host profile tables, `mdm_config_assets`, `jobs`, `host_disk_encryption_keys`, `setup_experience_status_results`, `mdm_windows_enrollments`.

Rather than leave the rest as a gap, `build/check-column-names.py` now covers every column reference the manual makes **inside a SQL block through a bound alias**: 146 distinct table-and-column pairs across 27 tables, from 99 SQL blocks, all present in `schema.sql` at this tag. Backtested by seeding three plausible wrong columns into this chapter; all three caught with correct line numbers and no false positives elsewhere. Provenance and the reason it works where the prose experiment did not are in `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

**What remains unverified** is the column inventory *tables* in prose, where a column is a backticked identifier in a markdown row rather than a reference in a query. Those are still carried forward from the previous pass, and they are the case `CONTRIBUTING.md` already records as not fixable by rule.

**Apple's 7-day DEP cursor expiry is not a Fleet fact** and is not verifiable here. Fleet's side, the detection and the full-fetch fallback, is verified; the number is Apple's.

**The Redis section is largely not verifiable at this tag.** The key patterns were checked against `server/live_query/redis_live_query.go` and the error store. The `client-output-buffer-limit pubsub 32mb 8mb 60` default is **Redis's**, not Fleet's, and the ElastiCache parameter names are AWS's. **§8.6.13's exclude-any guard behaviour** is described in 1.3 and was not re-derived here.

## Confirming review, 2026-08-27: READY WITH MINOR CHANGES, applied

Every substantive prior finding confirmed resolved. Two items, both introduced by the correction pass itself, which is the failure mode this project keeps generating.

**The profile tables' Extra columns lists were damaged by an earlier fix.** Moving `profile_uuid` into the Key column left it duplicated in Extra on both rows, and on the Apple row it had displaced `profile_identifier`, which is a real column and the one that matters when correlating with a payload. Both rows repaired against `schema.sql`.

**`apple_mdm` was described as a name appearing in two lists.** It is not: the schedule is `apple_mdm_worker`, and `apple_mdm` is the `jobs.name` value that schedule drains, doubling as its alternate lock name. The distinction is operational rather than pedantic, because `fleetctl trigger apple_mdm` gets you nothing, so the text now names both and says which one the trigger takes.

**Found while checking the above, not in the review:** 8.6.11 said a `Failed profile` message is "normal, not an error", which is the same overclaim the 8.14 confirming round had just corrected in the other direction. It is a real per-member failure that the command tolerates, expected when running from outside the server's environment, and still a member the recipient will not get. Two chapters were arguing; they no longer are.

### Premium markers on the schedule table, corrected 2026-08-27

Found by sweeping every licence claim in Part III after 3.5's confirming review caught one wrong in each direction, then extending the sweep here because the schedule table makes eleven licence claims by omission.

`registerPremiumCrons` registers eleven schedules and returns immediately without a Premium licence. The table marked nine of them and missed **`send_recovery_lock_commands`** and **`google_workspace_sync`**, both of which simply do not exist on Fleet Free. That matters in the same way the `apple_mdm_iphone_ipad_refetcher` case did in 3.5: a Free administrator looking for a `cron_stats` row for a schedule that was never registered has nothing to find and no indication why.

Checked and correct as-is: `host_vitals_label_membership` is registered by `registerMiscCrons`, not the Premium set, so its unmarked row is right.

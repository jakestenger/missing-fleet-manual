# 7.6 Maintain credentials, certificates, and privileged access, citation ledger

Drafted 2026-08-28. Research consensus in one round with seven corrections, two of which required
correcting a finished chapter. Draft, one review round applied. Held to Part VII's four-class
evidence rule ([`../part7-structure.md`](../part7-structure.md)).

## Operational practice

The renewal order for HTTPS trust, the secret-rotation sequence in both its overlapping and
non-overlapping forms, the access-review procedure, and the compromise sequence are judgement.

**Advisory intake is named as this chapter's to own**, with execution handed to 7.2. **Restored at
review**, where it had been given away entirely.

## Not established

**Re-establishing a readable Windows population after a certificate change.** Preserving and
verifying the old material first is certain. Fleet exposes **no single supported operation that
re-escrows an existing population on demand**, so the chapter tells a reader to plan that half against
their own deployment and verify by retrieval rather than assuming a command exists.

## Rejected, and why

**"Re-escrow before the change."** Killed at research: anything escrowed beforehand is encrypted to
the old certificate.

**"Back up the Apple certificate authority material separately from the database."** It is in the
database. What it needs is the key that decrypts it.

**Retrieval as proof of recovery.** It establishes service-side decryptability on that chain, not that
the population is readable and not that a credential would unlock a device.

**An access-review section that only listed what cannot be established.** True but not actionable; it
now says what to do first, and notes that where identities come from a provider, the provider's own
record is the authority for group membership rather than Fleet's.

## 2026-09-02 fix: blanket "Fleet does not alert on expiry" claim (round3 B5)

"Monitor expiry, advisories, and entitlement" said flatly that Fleet gives no advance warning for
any credential in the inventory table. Verified against `fleet-public` at `fleet-v4.90.1`:
`frontend/context/app.tsx` computes a 30-day expiry window for the APNs, Apple Business, and VPP
credentials, and `frontend/components/MainContent/MainContent.tsx:51-74` renders an app-wide banner
for whichever of those is closest to expiring (APNs on both editions, ABM/VPP on Premium), in
priority order. [2.10](../../manual/02-administer-and-deploy-fleet/2.10-apple-mdm-configuration.md)
already described this correctly for AB/VPP but the same chapter's own summary line contradicted
it. Rewrote the claim to distinguish the three Apple credentials (which do get a banner plus a
Renew date column) from everything else in the table (HTTPS certificate, server private key, MDM
CA, Windows enrolment identity, IdP/integration secrets, MCP tokens — genuinely no metric, webhook,
email, or in-app warning for any of those).

# Who Fleet is

Fleet Device Management builds software for the people responsible for keeping an organization’s computers useful, secure, and ready for work. Its ambition is to make that responsibility easier to carry: fewer disconnected tools, clearer information, and more time to improve the experience of the people you support.

Transparency runs through the way Fleet pursues that ambition. The software, the work that shapes it, and the handbook employees use to run the company are open to view. For an administrator choosing a platform that will reach across their organization, that openness offers something valuable: the ability to get to know both the product and the people building it.

## Open by default

Fleet makes its work visible on [GitHub](https://github.com/fleetdm/fleet) and its website. You can inspect the server and device-agent code, read the documentation, explore product plans, and see how an idea becomes a change. That gives you several ways to evaluate Fleet, whether you want to try the interface, understand a design decision, or have your security team examine how the software handles data.

This is a practical foundation for trust. When your organization depends on a feature, you can look into how it works. When you need an integration, your developers can inspect the interfaces and implementation. When something behaves unexpectedly, the public record gives your investigation a starting point. Fleet describes this commitment in its [approach to open-source device management](https://fleetdm.com/lp/open-source).

## Follow the work from request to release

Feature requests and bug reports have a public home in [Fleet’s GitHub issues](https://github.com/fleetdm/fleet/issues). The discussion can bring together the person who reported a problem, administrators with similar needs, designers working through the experience, and engineers building the change. You can contribute the details that explain why a request matters in a real environment.

As work progresses, issues, pull requests, reviews, and release notes make it possible to follow the implementation. A bug report can lead you to its fix; a feature discussion can show you the choices behind the finished behavior. Fleet’s [engineering handbook](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/engineering) describes how that work is tracked and reviewed.

For someone planning a rollout or an integration, this visibility is useful well before a release arrives. You can follow the work relevant to your plans, ask an informed question, and see how the scope develops. Fleet’s [product-design handbook](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/product-design#product-roadmap) also explains how feedback and release planning fit together.

## The employee handbook is public too

The [Fleet handbook](https://fleetdm.com/handbook) is the working guide employees use to run the company. It covers responsibilities, communication, product development, customer support, and the reasoning behind operating decisions. A prospective customer or contributor can read the same guidance that helps an employee understand how to get something done.

Fleet follows a handbook-first approach: changes to how the company works are documented before they become official. That gives a remote organization a shared place to find an answer and understand who owns a decision. The handbook itself can be improved through contributions, just like the documentation and code. [“Why this way?”](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/company/why-this-way#why-handbook-first-strategy) explains the practice.

Publishing this material lets you look beyond a product demonstration. You can learn how Fleet approaches a difficult decision, how its departments work together, and what kind of working relationship the company is trying to build with its customers.

## Values that shape the work

Fleet names five values in its [company handbook](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/company#values):

- **Empathy:** listen carefully and understand the person asking for help.
- **Ownership:** follow through, take responsibility, and consider long-term maintenance.
- **Results:** deliver useful improvements through practical choices and frequent iteration.
- **Objectivity:** investigate the evidence and reconsider decisions as the facts change.
- **Openness:** share work clearly, welcome contributions, and discuss disagreements respectfully.

These values give the public work a human purpose. An issue is someone’s blocked rollout or recurring support problem. A documentation improvement is an answer the next administrator can find for themselves. A clear explanation of a decision helps someone outside the company take part in it.

## Transparency for the people using devices

Fleet’s approach to openness also includes the person using a managed computer. Its [scope transparency guidance](https://fleetdm.com/docs/get-started/why-fleet#scope-transparency) emphasizes helping people understand what their organization can see and do on a device. That matters when you are asking employees to trust the tools that administer their working environment.

For an IT team, introducing Fleet is an opportunity to explain that relationship clearly: what you collect, why you need it, and how management helps keep the device ready for work. People should have a useful explanation of the software on their computer, along with somewhere to turn when they have questions.

## Built with a community

Fleet grew from the osquery community. The project began at Kolide in 2016, continued under community maintenance, and gained an independent company in 2020. Today, an all-remote staff works alongside contributors around the world. The [company history](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/company#history) follows those roots.

Fleet uses an open-core business model, with an open-source core and paid Premium features whose code is also available to inspect. There are many ways to take part: report a problem, describe a use case, improve an explanation, share a query, or contribute a change. Fleet’s [contribution philosophy](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/company/why-this-way#why-open-source) welcomes that participation.

That makes your experience operating Fleet useful beyond your own deployment. The question you raise today can improve the feature or documentation another administrator relies on tomorrow.

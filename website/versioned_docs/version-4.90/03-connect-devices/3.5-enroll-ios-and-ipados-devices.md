# Enroll iOS and iPadOS devices

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation-light.svg) Fleet lets you prepare company-owned iPhones and iPads for work and offer supported enrollment options for personal devices. These devices use Apple’s MDM channel for inventory and management. Once MDM is on, the management connection is ready; there is no fleetd installation to complete.

Fleet can display a device before its first MDM check-in. Apple Business synchronization creates Pending hosts, and the link-based enrollment exchange supplies serial, product, and UDID before enrollment completes. Use MDM status and subsequent device responses to confirm the connection, alongside the host record.

At this release, iOS and iPadOS have no osquery, live reports, scripts, or agent-collected vitals. Their capabilities follow the Apple management protocol and the enrollment path you choose.

Prepare the shared Apple connections in [2.10](../02-administer-and-deploy-fleet/2.10-apple-mdm-configuration.md), then choose one of the four paths below. For Macs, use [3.2](3.2-enroll-macos-devices.md).

<a id="the-decision-you-are-making"></a>

## Choose an enrollment approach

![Reference](../_assets/icons/reference-light.svg) **Who owns the device, and do you have it in Apple Business?**

| Path | Use it when | Fleet Premium? | What the console shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Automated Device Enrollment (ADE)** | Company-owned, purchased into Apple Business | **Yes.** Fleet Free refuses to upload, list, renew or count Apple Business tokens, so there is no ADE without Premium | On (company-owned) |
| **Enrollment URL, company-owned** | Company-owned, not in Apple Business, or already deployed | **Free to enroll**, Premium once you turn on end-user authentication to map an owner | On (manual) |
| **Enrollment URL, personal (BYOD)** | The device belongs to the person carrying it | **Free to enroll**, Premium once you turn on end-user authentication to map an owner | On (manual - personal) |
| **Account-driven user enrollment** | Personal devices, where you want the person to sign in with a **Managed Apple Account** rather than follow a link | **Yes** | On (manual - personal) |

The database and the API spell the first one `On (automatic)`, and the host filter spells it `automatic`; [8.6](../08-troubleshooting/8.6-server-state.md#one-status-three-vocabularies) has the mapping.

<a id="the-path-decides-whether-you-can-ever-lock-the-device"></a>

### Enrollment choices and remote lock eligibility

Choose the enrollment path with your remote lock and wipe requirements in mind. Changing that capability later requires re-enrollment:

| Path | Lock | Wipe |
|---|---|---|
| ADE | Yes | Yes |
| Enrollment URL, company-owned | **No** | Yes |
| Enrollment URL, personal (BYOD) | **No** | **No** |
| Account-driven user enrollment | **No** | **No** |

**Both are Fleet Premium wherever the table says yes.** Fleet Free refuses lock on every platform and refuses wipe on every platform except company-owned Android, so on these devices the table describes what Premium can do.

**Fleet refuses to lock any manually enrolled iPhone or iPad**, company-owned included, with a message saying so. That is narrower than the macOS rule, where only personal enrollment loses lock and erase. And personal enrollment on any Apple platform loses both, because the profile the device holds was built without those rights ([3.2](3.2-enroll-macos-devices.md)).

Use ADE when remote lock is required for an iPhone or iPad. Include Apple Business assignment in the purchasing and provisioning plan.

Personal enrollment removes lock and erase rights from the MDM profile on all Apple platforms. iPhones and iPads have an additional restriction: company-owned enrollment by link also lacks lock. macOS permits lock on its company-owned manual path ([3.2](3.2-enroll-macos-devices.md)).

Account-driven user enrollment is available for iOS and iPadOS at this release. Fleet accepts iPhone, iPad, and iPod product identifiers on that path and rejects Macs. The device may show only a generic failure to obtain an enrollment profile, so confirm platform support before offering the account-driven instructions.

## The enrollment URL, and what it does before MDM starts

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation-light.svg) The URL you hand out is your Fleet server's `/enroll` path with an enroll secret in it, and `byod=true` added when you picked personal. That is the whole configuration: no profile to build, no per-device work.

The link starts Apple’s over-the-air enrollment exchange, with two requests to the same endpoint:

1. The device presents a certificate chained to Apple's own CA. Fleet answers with a SCEP payload, and the device gets a keypair from it. **This keypair is not the identity certificate MDM will use later**; it exists only to sign the second request.
2. The device comes back signed with that certificate, and Fleet answers with an ordinary enrollment profile. Regular MDM enrollment proceeds from there.

The second request provides the serial number and hardware UUID. Fleet uses that information to create the host record, place it using the URL’s enroll secret, and associate the identity-provider account when personal enrollment with an IdP is configured.

On this link-based path, the enroll secret selects the initial fleet even though the device runs no agent. Plan link distribution and rotation around that scope:

- **Each link selects one placement scope**, because the secret in it belongs to one: a named fleet, or the global Unassigned scope, since an enroll secret may be global rather than fleet-scoped. That is not a limit of one link, as up to 50 secrets are supported, so several simultaneous links are supported for staged rotation or separate distribution channels. What it does mean is that handing one link to everybody puts everybody in the same place.
- **The link is a credential.** It carries an enroll secret in a query parameter, which means browser history, chat logs and help-desk tickets. [3.1](3.1-enrollment-design-and-host-lifecycle.md) covers rotation.

**Enrolling by link carries no licence check**, so the path itself is available on Fleet Free. Turning on end-user authentication, so that the link maps an owner, is a Premium setting; without it you get enrollment and no identity.

The enrollment page guides the employee through four steps. Include these in your rollout communication so they know that downloading the profile is followed by installation and trust prompts.

1. **Open the link in Safari.** The page tests the browser's user agent for Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Opera and Brave, and when it recognises one it shows an "Open in Safari" page with a copy fallback. That is a finite list rather than a Safari-only gate, so an in-app browser it does not recognise may go through instead of being stopped. Say "open this in Safari" in the message that carries the link rather than relying on the page to enforce it.
2. **Tap Download, then Allow** when iOS asks.
3. **Go to Settings, then Profile Downloaded, and tap Install.** iOS shows a red **Not Verified** warning at this point. It is expected, and saying so in advance is worth more than explaining it afterwards. Tap Install again when prompted.
4. **Tap Trust** on the Remote Management screen.

The page also tells the person what the ownership choice means for them: on the personal path, that the organisation can remove work data and settings but cannot wipe the device or lock them out; on the company-owned path, that the organisation can see and delete all device information. That is a useful thing to be able to quote when someone asks before installing.


## Automated Device Enrollment

<!-- SCREENSHOT-REUSE: SS-2.10-02. Reuse the Apple-token default-fleet selectors; extend that capture to include the BYOD selector if present in the release, because it governs account-driven enrollment. -->

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation-light.svg) For devices bought into Apple Business and assigned to Fleet as their MDM server. The sequence is the one [3.2](3.2-enroll-macos-devices.md) sets out, and the ADE-specific troubleshooting is [8.8](../08-troubleshooting/8.8-apple-mdm-diagnostics.md).

Each ABM token has independent default fleets for macOS, iOS, iPadOS, and BYOD. Devices arriving through Apple Business use the default for their platform. If an iPad lands in an unexpected fleet, check that token’s iPadOS default.

The BYOD default applies to account-driven user enrollment. Fleet identifies the token from the enrollment challenge and uses its BYOD destination. If it is unset, those devices land in Unassigned, regardless of the iOS and iPadOS defaults.

After enrollment, Fleet reconciles configuration profiles and, on Premium, installs setup-experience App Store apps. ADE waits for the required commands, profiles, and setup results before releasing the device. If Setup Assistant takes longer than expected, check that post-enrollment work as well as enrollment itself.

## Account-driven user enrollment

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation-light.svg) Account-driven user enrollment lets an employee enroll a personal device by signing in. It requires Fleet Premium, an identity provider, Managed Apple Accounts, and Apple Business service discovery.

**It needs Apple Business, even though the devices are personal.** Apple locates your MDM server by a *service discovery* URL registered against your Apple Business organisation, and Fleet registers that URL for you: the `mdm_service_discovery` schedule walks every ABM token you have uploaded once an hour, asks Apple what discovery URL it currently holds, and assigns Fleet's if it differs. With no ABM token uploaded it logs that there is nothing to do and stops, so **a new deployment cannot register a discovery URL and enrollment has no way to find you**. Read that as a statement about setting the path up today rather than about every working installation: this release still serves the service-discovery and account-driven endpoints without a token, and the sign-on callback accepts a challenge that names no token, so an enrollment established under an earlier arrangement can keep working.

Two consequences of it being registered rather than configured:

- **The discovery URL is derived from your Fleet server URL.** Change the server URL and the registered value is stale for up to an hour, until the schedule next runs and corrects it. `cron_stats` for `mdm_service_discovery` is where you confirm it ran ([8.6](../08-troubleshooting/8.6-server-state.md)).
- **Not every Apple Business organisation supports it.** When Apple says so, Fleet logs it and skips that token rather than failing, so the symptom is that one organisation's users cannot enroll while another's can.

Test both sign-ins used by the account-driven flow:

1. **To your identity provider.** The device's first request arrives without credentials, and Fleet answers with a `401` naming an SSO URL for it to open. Only after that sign-in does the device come back with a reference Fleet exchanges for an enrollment profile. There is no version of this path without an IdP configured ([2.5](../02-administer-and-deploy-fleet/2.5-identity-providers-sso-scim-and-role-sync.md)).
2. **To Apple, with a Managed Apple Account.** Fleet writes the assigned Managed Apple Account into the profile it returns, along with a BYOD enrollment mode. A personal Apple Account is not a substitute, and this is the half that a rollout planned around "an Apple ID and SSO" discovers late.

Account-driven user enrollment provides an enrollment identifier and user-scoped profiles. It omits the hardware serial and UDID as part of Apple’s privacy model, so these records cannot be reconciled directly against a hardware asset register. Personal enrollment by link supplies serial and UDID before enrollment, even though both paths display `On (manual - personal)`.

You can also map an owner during link-based enrollment by enabling Premium end-user authentication. The link sends the employee through your identity provider and associates the returned account with the host ([2.5](../02-administer-and-deploy-fleet/2.5-identity-providers-sso-scim-and-role-sync.md)). This provides owner mapping without the account-driven flow; a Free enrollment link provides enrollment without that identity step.

Account-driven enrollment adds user-scoped app licensing, so the license can follow the employee. Personal enrollment by URL remains device-scoped for App Store and in-house app delivery, just like company-owned manual enrollment. Both forms of app delivery require Premium: Free cannot upload Volume Purchasing tokens or deliver in-house apps.

## Verifying an enrollment

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto-light.svg) Confirm the MDM enrollment status in Fleet, then check that expected inventory and management responses arrive.

**The host appears, with an MDM status matching the path you used.** `Pending` means Apple Business has told Fleet about a device that has not enrolled yet, which is the expected state before someone unboxes it.

**Subsequent inventory detail is fetched over the protocol rather than by queries, and on Premium it arrives on a schedule rather than at enrollment.** Some of what you see predates that: an Apple Business sync can create a pending record, and the enrollment exchange itself supplies serial, product and UDID before MDM enrollment begins. Fleet asks the device for its inventory over MDM, and the asking is done by the `apple_mdm_iphone_ipad_refetcher` cron, which picks up freshly enrolled hosts on its next tick; enrollment itself deliberately does not trigger it, so a newly enrolled iPhone with a thin host page has not necessarily failed.

Use `cron_stats` for `apple_mdm_iphone_ipad_refetcher` to confirm that scheduled inventory collection ran. The host information supplied before enrollment is separate from those later device responses.

**That schedule is Premium-only.** Fleet registers it inside the Premium set and returns without registering anything when the licence is not Premium, so on Fleet Free it does not exist and there is no `cron_stats` row to look for. Since the enrollment link is the Free path, this combination is easy to walk into: a Free deployment enrolling iPhones by link has no periodic refetch and should not spend time troubleshooting a schedule that was never started ([8.6](../08-troubleshooting/8.6-server-state.md)).

Fleet has no shell or agent on these devices, so investigation usually begins with the command queue and Apple push channel ([8.8](../08-troubleshooting/8.8-apple-mdm-diagnostics.md)). For device-side evidence, collect a **sysdiagnose**, which includes the MDM client’s logs. [8.13](../08-troubleshooting/8.13-escalation.md) explains how to prepare that evidence for escalation.

## Who owns it afterwards

![Reference](../_assets/icons/reference-light.svg) **The APNs certificate**, which expires annually and takes every Apple device's MDM channel with it until it is renewed. An expiry is recoverable: renew the same certificate and delivery resumes, with nothing to do on the devices. **What is not recoverable is losing the Apple Account that created it**, since renewal requires that account and a new certificate breaks trust with every enrolled device. Store those credentials where more than one person can reach them. [2.10](../02-administer-and-deploy-fleet/2.10-apple-mdm-configuration.md) owns the renewal calendar.

**The ABM token**, if you use ADE, or if you are setting up account-driven enrollment, and its four default-fleet settings: macOS, iOS, iPadOS and BYOD.

**The enrollment links**, if you use them, because each one carries an enroll secret and secrets outlive the people who were handed them.

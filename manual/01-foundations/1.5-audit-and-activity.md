# Audit and activity

Fleet gives administrators a record of the change requested in Fleet and the result reported by the device. Reading those records together turns an endpoint action into an accountable operational story: what was requested, who requested it, how it progressed, and what the host reported.

## Activity records and host results

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation.svg) **Activity and audit events** are the administrative record. They show a Fleet configuration change or a request for work, such as assigning a profile, updating a policy, or initiating a script. They answer the questions an administrator asks first: who made the request, what was changed, and when did it happen?

**Host results and status** are the device record. A host can report software-install status, script output, policy status, or an MDM-command acknowledgement through the channel that carries that work. They answer the next question: what did this device report after Fleet made the work available?

Neither record replaces the other. A configuration change establishes intent; the host result shows its reported outcome. Together they let an operator move from an observed device state back to the responsible administrative action, or forward from a requested change to the devices that have reported a result.

## What the record covers, and where it stops

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation.svg) One boundary decides which questions this record can answer at all.

**Fleet records what Fleet did.** Every entry is written by the Fleet server at the moment it performs an action. Creating a policy, assigning a profile, running a script, transferring a host, changing someone's role, signing in.

**Fleet records nothing the device did on its own.** An action taken on the machine, outside Fleet, leaves no entry. Someone uninstalling the agent locally, deleting a profile by hand, turning off encryption at the keyboard, or another management tool removing a package are all invisible here.

That is the tool's scope working as designed, and it defeats the expectation most people bring to an audit trail. "How did the agent get removed from this laptop?" has no answer here unless Fleet removed it. The answer is on the device, in [8.4](../08-troubleshooting/8.4-host-side-investigation.md).

Set that expectation before someone runs the query, because an empty result looks suspicious when it is simply out of scope.

### Reading a secret is recorded as an action

[1.4](1.4-identity-and-roles.md) notes something uncomfortable: a read-only role at fleet scope can view disk encryption recovery keys. This is the other half of it.

Fleet treats retrieving a secret as an event in its own right. Reading a host's disk encryption key, viewing a recovery lock password, and reading a managed local account each write an entry naming who did it and when.

The read-only role can see the secret. It cannot see it quietly. That is what makes the support-recovery trade a reasonable one to accept.

### Sign-ins are recorded too, including the failures

Successful and failed sign-ins both appear, as `user_logged_in` and `user_failed_login`. A run of failures against one account is exactly the sort of thing that should reach the same place as the rest of your security events, which is what delivery, below, is for.

**There is no separate activity for a multi-factor prompt at this release.** If your monitoring needs to see second-factor events, they come from the identity provider rather than from Fleet, and that distinction is worth settling before anyone builds an alert on it.

### Upcoming activity: work still queued

A host carries **upcoming** activity as well as past activity: work Fleet has queued for it and is waiting to deliver.

A script that has not run is not necessarily a failure. It may be a device that has not checked in. Past activity tells you what happened, upcoming tells you what is still owed, and a queue that only grows is a strong signal that a device has stopped collecting its work. [8.4](../08-troubleshooting/8.4-host-side-investigation.md) treats queue depth as a diagnostic in its own right.

## When to reach for the activity record

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation.svg) Activity in the Fleet UI and API gives operators a convenient history of ongoing administration. Use it when reviewing a change window, confirming a recent configuration update, or handing an action to another administrator. Pair the activity record with the affected hosts, policy results, command status, or software activity to understand the full operational context.

This model is especially useful when an action is intentionally gradual. The activity record identifies the rollout and its scope; device results show the progress of that rollout across the selected hosts.

## Reading the record: in Fleet, by webhook, or streamed

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation.svg) The record itself is available on every Fleet deployment. What differs is how you reach it.

**In Fleet**, through the interface and the Activities API. This is the immediate view, it needs a Fleet account to read, and it is bounded by whatever retention you have configured.

**Pushed per event, through an activity webhook.** Fleet can POST each activity to a URL as it happens, configurable organization-wide or per fleet. This is the route for reacting to something rather than reviewing it later: a change you want to see in a chat channel, or an event that should open a ticket.

**Streamed to a log destination**, which is how the record ends up alongside your identity, infrastructure, and security events. This is a Fleet Premium capability, and it is the one that answers retention and correlation properly.

People reach for the third for one of three reasons, and the reason changes the answer. Retention, when the obligation outlasts what you want sitting in Fleet's database. Correlation, because a Fleet activity is far more useful next to identity events from the same ten minutes. Or access, when your security team needs to investigate without holding a Fleet account.

Decide who owns the destination, who may read it, and how long it is kept, before enabling anything. [2.5](../02-administer-and-deploy-fleet/2.5-activity-audit-logs-and-log-delivery.md) covers the setup.

## How long Fleet keeps the record

![Reference](../_assets/icons/reference.svg) Activity retention is configurable, and activities tied to a host are kept until the host itself is deleted. The expiry settings are off by default, so an untouched deployment keeps everything and the database grows to match. That is defensible. It should still be deliberate.

**If you need records for longer than you want them sitting in Fleet's database, retention and delivery are one decision rather than two.** Stream them out, then trim locally. Settle them separately and you end up exporting the evidence and keeping it as well. The settings are in [2.4](../02-administer-and-deploy-fleet/2.4-organization-and-server-settings.md).

## A review routine

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto.svg) Build regular review around a simple sequence:

1. Find the activity or audit event that created, changed, or requested the work.
2. Confirm the intended fleet and label scope.
3. Inspect the host results reported through the relevant device channel.
4. Record the decision to expand, adjust, or complete the work.

[8.12](../08-troubleshooting/8.12-audit-logs.md) is the reference: roughly two hundred activity types, the shape of an entry, and how to query the record when you are looking for what changed.

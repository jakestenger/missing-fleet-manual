# Audit and activity

Fleet gives administrators a record of the change requested in Fleet and the result reported by the device. Reading those records together turns an endpoint action into an accountable operational story: what was requested, who requested it, how it progressed, and what the host reported.

## Two records describe one piece of work

**Activity and audit events** are the administrative record. They show a Fleet configuration change or a request for work, such as assigning a profile, updating a policy, or initiating a script. They answer the questions an administrator asks first: who made the request, what was changed, and when did it happen?

**Host results and status** are the device record. A host can report software-install status, script output, policy status, or an MDM-command acknowledgement through the channel that carries that work. They answer the next question: what did this device report after Fleet made the work available?

Neither record replaces the other. A configuration change establishes intent; the host result shows its reported outcome. Together they let an operator move from an observed device state back to the responsible administrative action, or forward from a requested change to the devices that have reported a result.

## Use activity for operational context

Activity in the Fleet UI and API gives operators a convenient history of ongoing administration. Use it when reviewing a change window, confirming a recent configuration update, or handing an action to another administrator. Pair the activity record with the affected hosts, policy results, command status, or software activity to understand the full operational context.

This model is especially useful when an action is intentionally gradual. The activity record identifies the rollout and its scope; device results show the progress of that rollout across the selected hosts.

## Deliver audit events to the systems that retain them

Self-hosted Fleet deployments can deliver audit events to an external log destination. This lets an organization retain Fleet’s administrative record alongside identity, infrastructure, and security records according to its established logging and retention practices.

Decide which team owns the destination, who may read the events, and how long the organization retains them. Audit events are most useful when the organization can associate their Fleet identity with its broader identity and change-management records.

## Review both the request and the outcome

Build regular review around a simple sequence:

1. Find the activity or audit event that created, changed, or requested the work.
2. Confirm the intended fleet and label scope.
3. Inspect the host results reported through the relevant device channel.
4. Record the decision to expand, adjust, or complete the work.

The Audit logs reference and troubleshooting material provide the detailed event fields, delivery setup, and diagnostic workflow.

# Retire a Fleet deployment

**The operation nobody writes down until they need it.** Every other chapter in this part assumes the service has a future. This one is for the day it does not, and its job is to make sure the deployment ends by decision rather than by neglect.

Two steps lean on work other chapters define: the final backup is [7.2](7.2-back-up-and-restore-service-state.md)'s discipline, proven restorable and stored with its key material, and revoking what the deployment holds outward is [7.6](7.6-maintain-credentials-certificates-and-access.md)'s credential inventory, walked one last time.

## Retire a deployment deliberately

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto.svg) Six steps, ordered so that nothing is released before what it depends on is safe.

Shutting Fleet down is not deleting the server. Devices remain enrolled, credentials remain live, and external services still hold assignments made in your name.

A defensible order:

1. **Decide what must be retained**, and for how long: activity history, escrowed credentials, inventory. Export it while Fleet still runs, because afterwards it is a restore exercise.
2. **Release devices**, or hand them to whatever manages them next. Leaving them enrolled to a server that has stopped answering is the worst outcome for the people using them.
3. **Release external assignments**: enrolment profiles, purchased-app tokens, calendar and ticket integrations.
4. **Revoke credentials outward**, including anything held by an identity provider ([7.6](7.6-maintain-credentials-certificates-and-access.md)). If a Fleet MCP server pointed at this deployment, stop it and revoke its API-only token and client bearer too ([6.6](../06-automate-fleet/6.6-connect-fleet-to-an-ai-assistant.md)); neither expires on its own, so an unrevoked one keeps answering against a Fleet that is supposed to be gone.
5. **Take a final backup and store it with its key material**, because a database without the key retires nothing; it just becomes unreadable at a time of its own choosing.
6. **Then decommission**, and delete on your retention schedule rather than immediately.

**Steps two and three are the ones that hurt if skipped**, because they involve devices and services you no longer control.

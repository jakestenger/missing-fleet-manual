# Bind Android Enterprise

You supply nothing to connect Fleet to Android: no certificate, no key, nothing with an expiry date. You bind Fleet to a Google enterprise, Google issues the binding, and it never renews. What is hard to change afterwards is not a credential but your Fleet server URL: Fleet will accept a new one, and Google keeps pushing to the old one.

## Purpose and scope

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation.svg) This chapter covers connecting Fleet to Android Enterprise: the binding that stands in for a credential, what the connection commits you to afterwards, and how to turn it off. Enrolling individual devices is [3.6](../03-connect-devices/3.6-enroll-android-devices.md). What MDM asks of your deployment in general is [2.9](2.9-mdm-architecture-and-foundations.md).

Anyone arriving from Apple's model should set it aside here. Android uses no vendor-issued certificate on an annual clock, and it does not fail the way Apple fails.

The basic connection is Fleet Free, for work profiles on personally owned devices. Company-owned enrollment is **not licence-gated in Fleet's server at this release**: nothing in the Android module checks a licence, and a company-owned QR enrollment is one query parameter on the same token endpoint the personal path uses ([3.6](../03-connect-devices/3.6-enroll-android-devices.md)), so confirm the commercial side against Fleet's own pricing rather than against behaviour. Migrating Android hosts from another MDM is manual at both ends.

Windows' commitments are the inverse shape: a certificate you issue yourself, with no issuer behind it and an escrow consequence if you ever replace it ([2.11](2.11-configure-windows-management.md)).

## Android: binding an enterprise

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto.svg) There is no certificate to generate for Android. You bind Fleet to an Android Enterprise, and everything afterwards flows through Google.

Fleet supports devices that are Play Protect certified, previously known as GMS. Start at **Settings > Integrations > Mobile device management (MDM)** and select **Connect**, which opens Google's signup. Which flow you get depends on what your organization already uses, and the difference is mostly about proving you own your domain.

With **Google Workspace**, sign up with an existing admin account and a free Android Enterprise subscription is added to your Workspace. With **Microsoft 365**, enter your Microsoft email and choose **Sign in with Microsoft**. With **neither**, sign up with a work email rather than a personal `@gmail.com` address, and verify your domain in the Google Admin console afterwards.

**Whether Microsoft 365 organizations need to verify the domain is unclear at this release.** Fleet's guide states in one place that Google Workspace and Microsoft organizations do not need to verify domain ownership, and then instructs Microsoft 365 administrators to do exactly that as the last two steps of their procedure. The two readings cannot both be right and the source does not resolve it. Verifying the domain is harmless and satisfies either reading, so do it unless you already know your domain is verified.

**The Workspace account needs super administrator privileges, but only for a moment.** It is used for the initial enterprise binding and nothing else. Once the binding exists it survives, so the privileges can be reduced immediately afterwards and the connection keeps working.

## What the Android connection commits you to

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation.svg) Fleet does not talk to Google's Android Management API directly by default. It talks to a proxy running on fleetdm.com, which talks to Google. The enterprise creation, the policy, the Pub/Sub subscription and the per-device enrollment tokens all pass through that proxy.

Three consequences follow, and each one is easier to accept before enrollment than after.

**Your Fleet server URL must be publicly reachable.** Google delivers device events by Pub/Sub push, which means Google originating a connection to your server. A URL that only resolves inside your network cannot receive them.

**That URL is then fixed.** The Pub/Sub subscription is created against one URL and Fleet has no way to move it. Changing your Fleet server URL after Android is turned on breaks Android management, and there is an open Fleet issue tracking the gap. If a hostname change is anywhere in your plans, make it before you connect Android.

**One Fleet server URL supports one Android enterprise, on the default path.** This is enforced by the proxy Fleet operates, which is what a default deployment uses; a deployment configured to talk to Google directly is not subject to the proxy's rule. It is most visible to people running non-production instances, where the same URL cannot simply be rebound after the database is reset.

There is also a half-finished state worth knowing about. The binding takes several round trips, and a network failure partway through can leave the proxy having created an enterprise that your Fleet server never received the secret key for. The symptom is a connection that looks established on Google's side and absent on Fleet's.

## Android is managed without an agent, and then with a very small one

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation.svg) There is no fleetd on Android and no osquery, and the traffic is mainly Google telling Fleet what happened: `ENROLLMENT` when a device enrolls, `STATUS_REPORT` when its state changes.

**Two things stop that being the whole picture**, and both matter when something goes wrong. Fleet **polls Google hourly** to reconcile which devices still exist, so a disappearance can be noticed without anyone being told. And every host, on both editions, gets a **narrow Fleet app** added to its policy at enrollment, which enrols with Fleet and reports certificate results back once a certificate template exists ([3.6](../03-connect-devices/3.6-enroll-android-devices.md)); creating that certificate template in the first place is the Premium-gated step, not installing the app. So there is a small amount of Fleet-initiated traffic and a small device-side component.

Which means Android hosts sit outside most of what the rest of this book describes. No live queries, no scheduled reports, no scripts. Policies and inventory on Android come from what the Android Management API chooses to report, and the surface is Google's, not Fleet's.

The single exception is certificates, and the reason is a gap in Google's API rather than a Fleet design choice. The Android Management API has no REST call for installing a client certificate. What it does allow is delegating the `CERT_INSTALL` scope to a companion app, so Fleet ships one, and that app exists to do that one thing.

Fleet adds it to the device policy as `FORCE_INSTALLED`, so the user cannot decline it, with its permissions pre-granted, the `COMPANION_APP` role, and high-priority auto-updates. The policy also pins the app's signing certificate fingerprint, so a package that merely shares the name cannot take its place. Its package name and expected fingerprint are server settings, `mdm.android_agent.package` and `mdm.android_agent.signing_sha256`, which have working defaults, are hidden from the configuration reference, and must be set together or not at all. They exist for Fleet's own development, and there is no ordinary reason to touch them.

Android API operations are also batched, at `mdm.android_batch_size` hosts per call, defaulting to 100 with 0 meaning no limit. That one is likewise hidden, and it matters only if you are reasoning about API pressure on a large Android estate.

## Personally enrolled Android hosts report an enterprise-specific ID

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation.svg) On a personally owned Android device enrolled with a work profile, the `hardware_serial` and `uuid` that Fleet reports are not hardware identifiers. They are what Google calls an enterprise-specific ID: unique to this work profile in your organization, stable across factory resets, and meaningless to anyone else.

This is deliberate on Google's part and Fleet inherits it.

**Apple's personal enrollments are not all alike here, and the console does not distinguish them.** Both show "On (manual - personal)", and only one hides the hardware: **account-driven user enrollment** gives Fleet an enrollment identifier rather than a serial or UDID, while **personal enrollment by link** supplies the real serial and UDID during the over-the-air exchange ([3.5](../03-connect-devices/3.5-enroll-ios-and-ipados-devices.md)). So the status alone does not tell you whether a given Apple host's identifiers are real.

Any process that assumes a serial number identifies a physical device, hardware asset reconciliation especially, will not work on the Android hosts above or on account-driven Apple enrollments.

Wiping behaves differently too. Fleet sends the Android Management API's wipe command rather than its delete device command, specifically because the delete command expires after thirty days. A device that is off, lost or abroad for longer than that would otherwise quietly stop being wiped. The wipe command does not expire, so the work profile is removed whenever the device next appears.

## Turning Android MDM off deletes your enterprise

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto.svg) This off switch deserves more care than most, because it is not reversible in any ordinary sense.

**Turning Android MDM off deletes your Android Enterprise.** Fleet deletes the enterprise at Google, drops its own record of it, and marks every Android host unenrolled, so management stops for all of them. What happens on the devices afterwards is Google's side of the contract rather than something Fleet commands: on personally owned devices Google's behaviour is that the work profile goes too, and a fully managed company-owned device has no work profile to lose, it simply stops being managed. This is the intended behaviour, not an edge case, and it is not undone by turning Android MDM back on: that is a fresh binding.

The same thing happens if somebody deletes the Android Enterprise from the **Google Admin console**, without going near Fleet. Android MDM turns off in Fleet and the same device-side consequences follow. Whoever holds administrative access to that Google organization can end Android management for your whole estate from a console that has nothing to do with Fleet, and there is no confirmation step in Fleet to catch it.

## Verification and ownership

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto.svg) For Android, the settings page is genuinely the confirmation. **Settings > Integrations > MDM** shows Android MDM on once the binding is complete, and on the Google side the free Android Enterprise subscription appears in the Workspace subscriptions list.

Ownership is worth naming explicitly, because the binding puts a piece of your Fleet deployment inside somebody else's administrative boundary. The Android enterprise belongs to whoever administers your Google organization, with the consequences described above.

## Edge cases and precedence

![Reference](../_assets/icons/reference.svg) A few behaviours here surprise people, and all of them come from the boundary between Fleet and the platform vendor.

Android software installs must be marked self-service. Apps reach the user through the Play Store inside the work profile, so there is no silent push equivalent for Android.

Android operating system updates have no built-in form in Fleet at this release, unlike macOS, iOS, iPadOS and Windows. They can still be enforced: the control is a `systemUpdate` block inside an Android configuration profile, Premium-gated independently of everything else, and [5.6](../05-manage-devices/5.6-control-operating-system-updates.md) owns it.

## Version notes

![Reference](../_assets/icons/reference.svg) Verified against Fleet 4.90.0. Google's signup flow is vendor-owned and changes on Google's schedule, not Fleet's.

Certificate delivery to Android hosts arrived in 4.84.0, with automatic retry of failed installations and per-host resending; 4.85.0 added ordering so that a Wi-Fi profile referencing a certificate is delivered only after the certificate itself. An instance running an earlier release will not behave as described.

The Fleet server URL restriction on Android is a known limitation with an open issue against it, so it may not hold indefinitely. Everything else in this chapter reflects deliberate design.

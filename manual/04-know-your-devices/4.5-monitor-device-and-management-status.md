# Monitor fleet-wide state

Everything so far in this part reads one host, or one question across hosts. This chapter is about the estate: the counts Fleet computes, how to cut them into populations you can act on, how to get data out when the interface is the wrong tool, and how to be told about a problem rather than finding it.

Single-host diagnosis stays in Part VIII. What belongs here is the shape of the whole thing, and the discipline of not over-reading a number.

## The counts Fleet gives you, and what each one means

![Reference](../_assets/icons/reference.svg) Fleet computes a host summary you can request for the whole estate or for one fleet, and the counts in it are not all the same kind of thing.

| Count | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Total** | Hosts Fleet has a record for, in the scope you asked about |
| **Online** | Within its check-in window plus the grace period ([1.2](../01-foundations/1.2-how-fleet-reaches-a-device.md)) |
| **Offline** | Outside that window |
| **Missing in action** | No communication for **thirty days**, a fixed threshold rather than a setting |
| **New** | Recently enrolled |
| **Low disk space** | Hosts below a disk threshold you supply, between 1 and 100 |
| **Per platform** | A breakdown by platform token |
| **Automated enrollment errors** | Hosts whose Apple Business assignment failed |

Two of those need care.

**"Online" is an osquery judgement**, so it means much less for a device with no osquery. An iPhone, an iPad or an Android device is assessed by the same calculation and will not behave the way you expect ([4.1](4.1-understand-hosts-vitals-and-inventory.md)). Count mobile hosts separately rather than folding them into an estate-wide online figure.

**Low disk space is Fleet Premium, and Free does not refuse it.** Ask for it without a licence and Fleet **silently drops the parameter**, returning a summary with no such count rather than an error. That is a pattern worth recognising: a Premium capability presenting as an absence rather than a refusal. If you are building a report on this count, confirm your tier first.

## Cutting the estate into populations

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto.svg) A number for the whole estate is rarely actionable. Three tools narrow it, and they compose.

**Fleets** are the durable scope, and asking for a summary per fleet is the cheapest useful segmentation. Named fleets are Fleet Premium ([1.3](../01-foundations/1.3-hosts-fleets-labels.md)).

**Labels** are the flexible tool, and the right one for a population that crosses fleets or that you expect to dissolve: a hardware generation, an operating system version, a rollout ring. Built-in labels give you platform populations with no setup.

**Filters** on the host list narrow by status, platform, software title, policy result and the presence of an issue. This is where an investigation usually starts: filter to the population that looks wrong, then ask what its members have in common.

**The move worth learning is turning an observation into a label.** If a filter finds a set that matters, and it will matter again next week, make it a label so that next time the question is a lookup rather than a repeat investigation.

## Reading agent, enrollment, and management posture

![Explanation](../_assets/icons/explanation.svg) Four fleet-wide questions come up repeatedly, and each is a shape rather than a single number.

**Is the agent healthy everywhere?** The useful figure is not "online" but the gap between online and total, segmented by platform. A platform whose offline share is much larger than its neighbours' is telling you something structural rather than something about individual machines.

**Is everything that should be MDM-enrolled actually enrolled?** Compare the agent-enrolled population against the MDM-enrolled population, per platform, and look at the difference in **both** directions. Hosts with an agent and no MDM are one problem; hosts with MDM and no agent are a different one, and the second is the quieter of the two ([3.2](../03-connect-devices/3.2-enroll-macos-devices.md)).

**Are agent versions converging?** Look for a tail rather than an average. A long tail on an old fleetd version has a small number of causes, which [3.7](../03-connect-devices/3.7-manage-fleetd-orbit-and-updates.md) enumerates; what this chapter wants is the count and whether it is shrinking.

**Is anything failing to enroll automatically?** The automated enrollment error count is the one signal here that points at Apple Business rather than at your own configuration ([2.7](../02-administer-and-deploy-fleet/2.7-apple-mdm-configuration.md)).

## Two kinds of history, and a trap in the name

![Reference](../_assets/icons/reference.svg) Fleet keeps a small amount of historical data in two datasets, and the first has a name that invites the wrong reading.

**Uptime history does not record how long hosts have been up.** It records **which hosts were online**, in time buckets. The host record does have a boot-duration `uptime` field, and this history is not a history of that field. Read it as a presence chart: which machines were around, and when.

**Vulnerability exposure history** tracks critical-severity findings over a curated set of software ([4.4](4.4-understand-software-and-vulnerabilities.md)).

Both share the same limits. Buckets are a few hours wide by default rather than continuous. Retention is about a month. The default view covers the last week. So this is a trend indicator rather than an audit record: it answers "is this getting better or worse", and it does not answer "what was true on the fourteenth".

## Reading at scale, when the interface is the wrong tool

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto.svg) Three routes out, with different strengths.

**The API** is the route for anything repeated. Host lists are paginated, and the filters available in the interface are available as parameters, so a saved investigation becomes a scripted one. The host summary endpoint returns the counts above for a scope, which is cheaper than listing hosts and counting them yourself.

**CSV export** is the route for handing a population to somebody who does not use Fleet. The filter is the work; the export is the easy part.

**`fleetctl`** is the route for a person at a terminal. Note what it is not: it does not retrieve stored report results ([4.2](4.2-run-queries-and-reports.md)), so a script that needs those wants the API.

**Ask for the narrowest thing that answers the question.** Requesting every host and filtering locally works until it does not, and the point where it stops working tends to be a production incident rather than a slow script ([8.14](../08-troubleshooting/8.14-degradation.md)).

## Being told, rather than looking

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto.svg) Fleet can send a webhook when too much of the estate goes quiet, and this is the one fleet-wide alert most deployments should have.

The host status webhook takes four settings: whether it is enabled, a destination URL, a **percentage of hosts**, and a **number of days**. It fires when at least that percentage of hosts has gone unseen for at least that many days.

**Both numbers need thought, and they need to match how your estate actually behaves.** A percentage set too low fires every weekend; set too high, it only fires once the problem is obvious anyway. An office fleet that goes quiet on Saturdays needs a different threshold from a server fleet that should never go quiet at all.

**It answers exactly one question: has a large part of the estate stopped talking to Fleet.** That is the failure most worth catching automatically, because it is the one that makes every other number quietly wrong. It will not tell you about a single important host, and it is not a substitute for policy automation ([4.3](4.3-use-policies-for-compliance.md)).

## A recurring review worth the half hour

![How-to](../_assets/icons/howto.svg) A sequence that surfaces most estate-level problems and can be worked on a schedule.

1. **Total against last time.** A total that moved sharply is a rollout or a deletion, and both are worth knowing about deliberately.
2. **Online share per platform.** Compare platforms against each other rather than against an absolute target.
3. **The missing-in-action population.** Thirty days of silence is a decision point: recover the host, or retire the record.
4. **Agent and MDM enrollment gaps**, in both directions, per platform.
5. **Agent version spread**, looking for a tail rather than a mean.
6. **Policy compliance**, counting the no-answer group rather than only the failures ([4.3](4.3-use-policies-for-compliance.md)).
7. **Known-exploited and critical vulnerabilities**, ordered by host count ([4.4](4.4-understand-software-and-vulnerabilities.md)).
8. **Anything that changed shape** rather than value. A count stable for a month that moved this week is more interesting than one that has always looked wrong.

**Record the decision, not only the observation.** A review that produces no decision leaves no evidence next month that anything was looked at.

## When a number does not make sense

![Troubleshooting](../_assets/icons/troubleshooting.svg) Rule out the reasons a count can be both right and surprising before escalating.

| Symptom | Consider first |
|---|---|
| A count is lower than expected | Scope. A fleet summary is not the estate, and a global one includes hosts you may not think of as yours |
| A whole platform looks unhealthy | Whether that platform's data comes from osquery at all ([4.1](4.1-understand-hosts-vitals-and-inventory.md)) |
| A count disappeared entirely | A Premium field on a Free deployment, dropped rather than refused |
| Counts disagree with the host list beneath them | Cached aggregates with their own timestamps, especially for policies ([4.3](4.3-use-policies-for-compliance.md)) |
| Software or vulnerability counts are zero | The inventory switch and the feed state, before anything else ([4.4](4.4-understand-software-and-vulnerabilities.md)) |
| Everything looks worse since a particular date | An upgrade, a settings change or a fleet reorganisation. The activity record dates those ([1.5](../01-foundations/1.5-audit-and-activity.md)) |

Once those are ruled out, an estate-wide problem is usually a server-state question ([8.6](../08-troubleshooting/8.6-server-state.md)), or a degradation question if it correlates with load ([8.14](../08-troubleshooting/8.14-degradation.md)).

# Who Fleet is

Fleet Device Management is the company behind Fleet. It aims to make device management approachable, reduce overlapping tools, and give IT and security teams more time for improvements. [Fleet’s company handbook](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/company#purpose) describes that purpose in its own words.

## A company with open-source roots

Fleet grew from the osquery community. The project began at Kolide in 2016, became community-maintained, and gained an independent company in 2020. An all-remote staff now works alongside contributors worldwide. [Fleet’s history](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/company#history) follows that progression.

Fleet uses an **open-core** business model: an open-source core alongside paid Premium features. Its Premium code is also available to inspect. The public work extends to documentation, the website, and the handbook that describes how the company runs. You can read the reasoning behind decisions as well as the resulting software. [“Why this way?”](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/company/why-this-way#why-open-source) explains the approach.

## Values that shape the work

The company names five values in its [handbook](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/company#values):

- **Empathy:** listen carefully, understand someone’s situation, and make helping them a priority.
- **Ownership:** follow through, take responsibility for mistakes, and consider long-term maintenance.
- **Results:** deliver useful improvements through small iterations and keep the work practical.
- **Objectivity:** examine the evidence and reconsider a decision when the facts change.
- **Openness:** make work understandable and visible, and welcome contributions and respectful disagreement.

For an administrator evaluating Fleet, these are useful expectations to bring to a conversation: a clear explanation, room for questions, and a willingness to investigate what is happening in your environment.

## Transparency for the people using devices

Device management reaches into someone’s working day. Fleet’s stated approach is to collect the information needed to administer and secure devices, while helping people understand what their organization can see and do. Its [scope transparency guidance](https://fleetdm.com/docs/get-started/why-fleet#scope-transparency) describes that intent. When you introduce Fleet, explaining your organization’s use of it is part of helping people feel informed about their work computer.

## Taking part

You can contribute through a bug report, a documentation correction, a question, or an improvement to the code. Fleet’s public development process gives those contributions a place to be discussed and reviewed; you can also read existing discussions to understand a decision or follow an issue that affects you. The handbook describes [how contributions reach the project](https://fleetdm.com/handbook/company/why-this-way#why-open-source).
